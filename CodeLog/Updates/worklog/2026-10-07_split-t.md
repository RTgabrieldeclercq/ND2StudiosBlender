## What changed

**Split T** (`util.split_t`) and **Select Frame** (`util.select_frame`) join the tap family as the T-axis twins of Split Z / Select Plane: the split card grows `t0…` sockets labelled with the frame time (`3 · 90.0 s`), a wired `tK` materializes into one shared `util.select_frame` tap, and Select Frame keeps one timepoint (default 0, the `frame` instant pick adopts the Viewer's t) with the per-T lists following through `time_subset`, `dt_s` kept exactly as Crop's frames mode keeps it, lattice layers and structure rows following, a t-only sampling stamp, refusal past the end with the series length, identity on a single frame; `metadata.frame_pick` / `select_frame` keep the card and the pull in agreement. Two rules now apply to EVERY split card: a **fan-out cap** (`document.FANOUT_CAP` = 24) above which a card grows no per-index sockets — only `out` — and the `groups` text becomes the way to split; and **`every N`** (`/N`) in that text, consecutive chunks of N from 0 (`metadata.every_n`; `parse_groups(raw, n)` expands it when the axis length is known, `ops.split_group` computes chunk k as k·N…k·N+N-1 without it, unclamped, because Crop's frames mode drops what lies past the end and so the last chunk clips itself). The four `groups` descriptions were rewritten to say all this and to state honestly that a group reaching past the end is clipped at the pull and only a group entirely past it is refused (the earlier wording claimed refusal). Roles (+ `op_pages` so both T nodes are offered on the refine page), demos, MANUAL §15 rows (Transform & utility 17) and the §8b pick table, a selftest and a probe block.

## Why

The brief from the other session: add Split T as the T-axis twin of Split Z, mirroring Split Z → Select Plane exactly, and decide ONE fan-out rule for T that Z uses too, because T is hundreds of frames and per-frame sockets are unusable past ~20. The rule chosen: per-index sockets up to 24, none above, groups beyond — one rule for every axis so Split Z on a 60-plane stack and Split T on a 300-frame series behave alike, and the `groups` text (already on the other three cards) is the single way to split a long axis rather than a second mechanism (a `more…` band would still be 300 ports somewhere). `every N` is the shorthand a long series actually needs — windows of ten frames — and it is cheap only because Crop clips: the run side needs no axis length. `dt_s` is kept rather than dropped (the brief suggested Stack's rule) because Select Frame keeps one REAL frame and a Split T group is a Crop keeping `t3`, so the two ways of picking frame 3 must stamp the same metadata; Stack drops `dt_s` because it merges frames and no interval is left. The default frame is 0 rather than the middle: the first frame is the acquisition's anchor (Registration's `first`), where the middle plane of a stack is the plane most likely in focus.
