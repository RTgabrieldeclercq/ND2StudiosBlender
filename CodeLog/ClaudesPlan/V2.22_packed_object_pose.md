# V2.22 — Packed-object segmentation, pose tracking, and surface-landmark binding

## Context

We need a node workflow that ingests an `.nd2` of a bed of fluorescent granules and outputs, per granule, a volumetric representation with tracked position **and rotation**, plus green surface dots bound to their parent F-granule and tracked. Granules are superellipsoids with surface roughness, constant interior fluorescence, in two color populations (F and I); only F granules carry the dots. The hard part is that granules **contact** each other, so intensity can never separate two touching same-color solids — the split has to come from geometry.

Nothing in the repo does parametric shape fitting, object orientation, soft per-voxel assignment, object classification, or parent/child binding. Those five gaps are this project. What *does* exist and gets reused is the whole points → clusters → mesh → label-volume → bands chain, `analysis.segment` (incl. CellSAM), `track.link`, `analysis.measure`, and the `bead_detect` LoG detector.

### Confirmed decisions

1. **Dimension-generic** (2D now, 3D later) behind `DimMode`.
2. **Auto-detect F vs I**: classify on `log(R-B / Nile Blue)`, then F := whichever population actually carries GFP dots. Two independent subsystems must agree; disagreement is reported, not hidden.
3. **Full stack as designed** — the superellipsoid optimizer, Gibbs centroid prior, probability field, competitive growth, surface-energy annealing and EM relaxation are all committed scope. Increments below are a dependency order with a green gate at each step, not a scope reduction.
4. **CellSAM → foreground only; EDT h-maxima → instances.** CellSAM is 2D-only, returns labels with no probability (it eats the decoder logits internally), and is trained on membrane-bounded cells, so it merges exactly at the contacts that matter. For a jammed bed of known-size convex solids the EDT interior maxima *are* the centroids and the contacts *are* the saddles. Run both, reconcile (EDT splits merged CellSAM components), gate on the expected geometric count.

## Architecture

CellSAM answers *granule or background*. The EDT answers *how many and where*. A parametric superquadric answers *what shape, which way is it facing*. The green dots answer *which way is it facing, absolutely, with no symmetry ambiguity*. The per-voxel probability is a Gibbs posterior over signed distance to the fitted surfaces. The whole thing is block-coordinate descent (EM) whose E-step is that posterior and whose M-step is the probability-weighted shape refit.

**This is what resolves the circularity in the original method.** "Assign centroids from the probability" + "probability from the centroids" is a fixed point with no initializer and no proof of existence. Written as EM on one explicit energy it becomes well-posed: initializer = EDT seeds → closed-form moment fits (needs no probability); an E-step and two M-steps that each provably reduce the same scalar; a termination test. The split/merge moves are the "clustering algorithm that relaxes into the most probable configuration" — accepted iff total energy decreases.

### Three corrections to the specified method

1. **Annealing `T` alone does nothing.** With the energy written under a single `1/T`, `argmin_k E_k` is `T`-independent, so annealing `T` leaves the MAP labeling *exactly unchanged* — it only softens the reported probability. "Relax the strictness" needs **two** schedules: `T` for the visualized field and the EM soft weights, and `(γ̃, τ)` for the MAP. Without this you build something that appears to relax and never moves its answer.
2. **Growth must be competitive, not per-granule.** Independent growth of K granules in a contacting bed yields overlapping volumes and a probability that does not normalize. Multi-source competition on a shared landscape is required, and growth must be able to *lose* voxels.
3. **Half the contacts are free.** With two colors and ~6 contacts per granule, roughly half of all contacts are cross-color and separated by the intensity term alone. Only F–F and I–I contacts need the geometry. This halves the central difficulty and should be stated in the docs.

### The energy (one scalar, everything minimizes it)

```
E_k(v) = s_k(v)  +  λ_I·½(I(v)−µ_c(k))ᵀΣ_c⁻¹(I(v)−µ_c(k))·ℓ  −  ℓ·log π_k
E_∅(v) = m₀                                    (background class — REQUIRED, see below)
p_k(v) = exp(−E_k/T) / (exp(−E_∅/T) + Σ_{j∈cand(v)} exp(−E_j/T))
```
`s_k` = signed radial distance to fitted surface k, in µm. `T ≈ σ_PSF + σ_rough`, in µm, physically the surface localization uncertainty. `ℓ = σ_PSF` converts the dimensionless intensity term to µm.

- `argmax_k p_k = argmin_k s_k` = nearest-surface assignment — the provably correct estimator for two identical touching convex solids (decision surface = the median plane).
- Honest at contacts: `s₁ = s₂ ⇒ p = 0.5`. Anything reporting 0.99 on a contact plane is lying.
- **The background class `E_∅` is not optional.** Without it the softmax normalizes over granules only, so a voxel 50 µm from everything gets `p = 0.34, 0.33, 0.33` — spuriously confident it belongs to *some* granule.

Total objective: `Σ_v E_{L(v)}(v) + γ̃|∂L| + µ·Σ max(0, s−τ)² + U({t})`.

### Surface energy — "no high-aspect-ratio juts" as a derived weight

Boundary-area Potts term `E_area = γ̃ Σ w_uv·1[L(u)≠L(v)]`, with metric-correct `w_u = 1/‖x_u−x_v‖` on anisotropic voxels. A protrusion of radius `w`, length `ℓ` gains `ΔV = πw²ℓ` and costs `ΔA = 2πwℓ`, so cost per unit volume gained is `γ̃·ΔA/ΔV = 2γ̃/w → ∞ as w → 0`. Any protrusion thinner than `w_min = 2γ̃/|s|_gain` is strictly rejected, so:

```
γ̃ = w_min · |s|_gain / 2      with w_min = 0.15·R̄,  |s|_gain = 3σ_PSF
```

That **derives the penalty weight from a physical statement** instead of tuning a constant. Log the implied `w_min` so the user sees what is being forbidden. Paired with a shape-slack hinge `µ·Σ max(0, s_k−τ)²` where `τ = 3σ_rough` is **measured from the fit residual**, not a free knob — that hinge *is* the user's "relax the strictness to allow variation in features".

Annealing (two schedules, 6 stages, `q = 0.7`):
```
T_i = T₀q^i,  T₀ = 3(σ_PSF+σ_rough), T_end ≥ 0.5·max(d)     # reported softness
τ_i = τ₀ + (τ_max−τ₀)·i/(n−1),  τ₀ = 0.5σ_rough → 3σ_rough   # strict → relaxed
γ̃_i = max(γ̃₀q^i, γ̃_min),  γ̃_min = w_min·3σ_PSF/2            # FLOORED
```
`γ̃` is floored at the calibrated value so **no schedule stage can ever admit a jut thinner than `w_min`** — the guarantee survives annealing.

### Memory — the constraint that dictates the representation

Per `(m,t,c)` unit this data is `7×1024×1024 = 7.34 M` voxels with `K ≈ 400` per plane. Dense `K×Z·Y·X` f32 = 11.7 GB — impossible. Use **top-M sparse**: `ids (Z,Y,X,M) uint16` + `p (Z,Y,X,M) uint8`, M=3 (a triple point is real; ≥4-way is measure-zero and folds into `p_other = 1−Σp`).

| Representation | Per `(m,t)` |
|---|---|
| dense K×Z·Y·X f32 | 11.7 GB — impossible |
| top-M sparse, M=3 | **66 MB** |
| top-1 + margin (default export) | **22 MB** |

Compute per z-plane over a **bounded candidate set** from a `cKDTree` on centroids at radius `2R̄+2σ_PSF` (contact number 6–12 in a jammed bed; cap `M_cand = 24` and log overflow). Working set = one plane × 24 f32 = 100 MB, matching the existing per-z-plane discipline in `granule_volume_mask.build_granule_masks`. Never materialize globally. `K > 65535` → raise (uint16 exhausted).

### `rotation.py` — build this FIRST, before anything touches a volume
Pure math, exactly testable, and every downstream correctness claim depends on it. Covers **SO(2) and SO(3)** per the dimension-generic decision.

```python
def symmetry_group(fit, *, tol_axis=0.03, tol_eps=0.05) -> SymmetryGroup
@dataclass SymmetryGroup: kind: str; mats: np.ndarray; free_axis; n_free: int
def geodesic_angle(R1, R2) -> float
def reduced_angle(R1, R2, group) -> float          # min over g of geodesic_angle(R1, R2@g)
def align_to_reference(R, R_ref, group) -> (R_aligned, g_index)
def angular_velocity(quats, dt, group) -> (omega, alpha)
def kabsch(P, Q, w=None) -> (R, rms, cov_delta)
def quat_from_matrix / matrix_from_quat / quat_mul / quat_canonical
def exp_so3 / log_so3                              # Taylor branch for θ < 1e-8; θ≈π eigenvector branch
```

**Fit parameterization: `R = R_ref·exp([δ]×)`, fitting the 3-vector `δ`, retracting between outer iterations.** Euler angles gimbal-lock and interact badly with `least_squares` bounds; a free 4-param quaternion makes `JᵀJ` singular along the radial direction. The local `so(3)` increment has exactly 3 DOF, no singularity for `‖δ‖ < π`, and is best-conditioned at `δ = 0`, which is where you always are by construction. **Store** as canonical quaternion (`w ≥ 0`) plus cached `R`.

**Symmetry is shape-dependent, and getting it wrong is the difference between a working tracker and garbage:**

| Condition (3D) | Group | Order | Unobservable DOF | Nyquist `|ω|Δt` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `a≠b≠c` | `D₂` | 4 | 0 (4 branches) | < 45° |
| `a≈b≠c, ε₂≠1` | `D₄` | 8 | 0 | < 22.5° |
| `a≈b≠c, ε₂=1` | `SO(2)_z` | ∞ | 1 (azimuth) | — |
| `a≈b≈c, ε₁=ε₂` | octahedral `O` | 24 | 0 | < 45° |
| `a≈b≈c, ε₁=ε₂=1` | `SO(3)` | ∞ | 3 | — |

2D superellipse: `a≠b → C₂` (order 2, Nyquist < 90°/frame); `a≈b, ε≠1 → C₄`; `a≈b, ε=1` (circle) → `SO(2)`, angle unobservable.

**Every rotation error metric in this project must be `reduced_angle`, never `geodesic_angle`** — a correct fit may legitimately return any of the `|G|` branches, so a naive quaternion distance fails ~75% of the time on correct code, and the natural response is to loosen the threshold until it passes, which destroys the test. `tol_axis` must come from the fit's own uncertainty (`3σ_a/a` from `cov_pose`), OR'd with a data-driven test (largest eigenvalue of the `δδ` block > (10°)²). Above Nyquist, aliasing is mathematically irrecoverable from body shape alone — surface it as a computed `ALIASING_RISK` flag, do not hide it.

### `superquadric.py`
2D superellipse and 3D superellipsoid behind one API.
```python
@dataclass(frozen=True) SuperquadricFit:
    center_um, R, quat, semi_axes_um, eps, volume_um3, rms_um, sigma_rough_um,
    harmonic_power, cov_pose, n_points, n_contact_points, flags, dims
def inside_outside(points_um, center_um, R, semi_axes_um, eps) -> (N,)
def signed_radial(points_um, fit) -> (N,) µm
def signed_radial_grid(fit, bbox, voxel_size_um) -> f32 grid
def surface_mesh(fit, n_eta=48, n_omega=96) -> (verts, faces)
def body_surface_coords(points_um, fit) -> (N,2) eta, omega
def moment_init(points_um, weights, r_known_um) -> SuperquadricFit    # closed form, NO optimizer
def fit_superquadric(points_um, weights=None, init=None, *, r_known_um, params=None,
                     jac="analytic") -> SuperquadricFit
```
Residual is the **signed radial distance in µm**, not `(F−1)`: raw `F−1` is badly scaled (it varies by orders of magnitude with `ε` and with distance) so LM weights far points absurdly. Use `s = ‖u‖(1 − F(u)^(−ε₁/2))`. Analytic Jacobian is required, not optional (~5 ms/granule with it). Exponents are non-convex → **grid over `(ε₁,ε₂)` then refine** (16-point grid × 25 LM iterations). Regularize the high-aspect-ratio degeneracy with `ρ_aspect` plus `ρ_size` on the absolute scale, letting eigenvalue *ratios* carry the shape — ratios survive contact truncation, absolute eigenvalues do not.

**Roughness is a residual statistic, not a second model**: `sigma_rough_um` = robust std of the fit residual, optionally with spherical-harmonic power `P_l`. Use `soft_l1` loss so roughness cannot drive `ε`, and report `ε` **with its covariance**, falling back to `ε=1` when the covariance is large — expect `ε` to be unidentifiable once `σ_rough > 0.15 R̄`.

`body_surface_coords` inverts Barr in closed form with signed powers `p^[e] ≡ sgn(p)|p|^e`: `ω = atan2((y/b)^[1/ε₂], (x/a)^[1/ε₂])`, `η = asin(clip((z/c)^[1/ε₁],−1,1))`. `(η,ω)` is the right **identity** coordinate for a dot across frames because it is invariant to small frame-to-frame drift in fitted `a,b,c,ε`, which Cartesian body coords are not.

Never raise for one bad granule — `least_squares` non-convergence → fall back to `moment_init` with `flags |= FIT_FAILED`; `N < 60` → `LOW_N`; coplanar → `DEGENERATE_RANK`. Raise only on structurally invalid input.

### `shape_probability.py`
```python
@dataclass GranuleProbField: ids (Z,H,W,M) uint16; p (Z,H,W,M) uint8; m_stored; voxel_size_um;
                             temperature_um; top1(); margin(); prob_of(gid)
def build_prob_field(fits, shape, voxel_size_um, *, intensity=None, class_of=None, params=None,
                     progress_cb=None) -> GranuleProbField
def centroid_prior_energy(centroids_um, radii_um, params) -> (U, grad)
def project_centroids(centroids_um, radii_um, params) -> (centroids, merge_pairs, info)
```
The **Gibbs centroid-spacing prior**, polydisperse (per-object radii, per the measured 4× size range):
```
r_hard = (R_k+R_l)(1−o_max), o_max=0.15;  r₀ = R_k+R_l (tangency);  r₁ = 1.3(R_k+R_l)
φ(r) = +∞                                    r < r_hard
     = w_rep·((r₀−r)/(r₀−r_hard))²           r_hard ≤ r < r₀     (repulsion)
     = w_att·((r−r₀)/(r₁−r₀))²               r₀ ≤ r < r₁          (weak cohesion — bed is packed)
U({t}) = Σ_{k<l} φ(r_kl),  pairs from cKDTree.query_pairs(r₁)
```
`w_rep ≈ 5 µm`, `w_att ≈ 0.5 µm`. Applied both as residual rows `√φ` in the joint fit (Gauss-Newton wants roots) and as a projection between EM steps, capped at 0.25 voxel of movement per step. **The prior does double duty as model selection**: two centroids at `r < r_hard` → forbidden → merge proposal; one centroid where packing wants two → huge residual and `V ≈ 2V̄` → split proposal.

### `shape_grow.py`
```python
def grow_competitive(fits, seeds, foreground, voxel_size_um, *, intensity=None, td=None,
                     params=None, method="watershed", progress_cb=None) -> (labels, prob, info)
def icm_refine(labels, prob, fits, voxel_size_um, *, gamma_um, tau_um, mu, n_sweeps=5, ...)
def dijkstra_reference(...)     # exact path-cost; small volumes and tests only
```
Landscape `Λ(v) = min_{k∈cand(v)} s_k(v)` flooded from eroded fitted interiors `S_k = {v : s_k < −0.35R̄}` by `skimage.segmentation.watershed(Λ, markers, mask=fg)`. Flooding `Λ` puts the divide exactly where two `s_k` cross — the median plane. Anisotropy is already inside `s_k` (µm coords), which is why a connectivity-based flooder is adequate. Rejected: multi-phase level sets (K coupled fields = the memory bomb, and it would re-derive a shape model we already have parametrically); pure Python Dijkstra as the main path (~400 s at 40 M contested voxels) — ship it as the **reference implementation** for tests.

ICM sweeps the **margin-gated contested set** `Ω = {v : p₁−p₂ < 0.5}` (~3–7% of voxels), checkerboard-vectorized, with a **topology guard** (26-conn simple-point test) rejecting any change that would disconnect a granule — without it, growth sheds islands and ICM happily creates them. Monotone at fixed `(γ̃,τ,T)`; **assert monotone energy decrease within a stage** in a test, and log-only across stages (the objective itself changes).

`λ_TD` term is a **recommended addition the data supports**: brightfield shows granule *outlines including at contacts*, where fluorescence has no signal at all because both solids are uniformly bright. It is the only direct observable of a contact plane in this dataset. Leave the hook with `λ_TD = 0` if synth does not model TD.

### `shape_relax.py` — the EM driver
```python
def relax_shapes(foreground, seeds, intensity, voxel_size_um, *, r_known_um, params=None,
                 progress_cb=None) -> GranuleSolution
@dataclass GranuleSolution: fits, labels, prob, class_of, energy_trace, info
```
```
init: fits = moment_init(seed voxels)                        # closed form, no probability needed
for stage i in 0..5:
  E-step:   prob = build_prob_field(fits, T=T_i, class_of, intensity)
  M-step-1: fits_k = fit_superquadric(bbox_k points, weights=p_k(v), init=fits_k, r_known_um)
            ^ this IS "centroid from the probability, weights controlled by known geometry"
  M-step-2: centroids = project_centroids(...)               # Gibbs prior
  labels:   grow_competitive(...) ; icm_refine(γ̃_i, τ_i)
  moves:    split if rms_k > 3·median OR V_k > 1.6V̄ ; merge if r_kl < r_hard OR joint fit wins
            accept iff total energy decreases
  stop when max|Δt_k| < 0.25·max(d) and label-change fraction < 1e-3
```

### `landmark_pose.py` — the rotation observable
```python
@dataclass Dot: id, parent_id, pos_um, body_um, eta, omega, s_um, intensity, flags
def detect_dots(gfp, voxel_size_um, *, r_dot_um, params) -> (points_um, rows)
def bind_dots(points_um, fits, class_of, *, r_off_um=None, params) -> (list[Dot], info)

def pose_from_dots(dots_by_frame, fits_by_frame, group, params) -> (quats, cov, n_used, flags)
```
**Reuse `kernels/bead_detect.detect_beads`** — it already has 3D LoG (`_fspecial_log`), `_subpixel_poly_3d` parabolic refinement, radial-symmetry refinement and NMS (`_suppress_close`). Do not write a new detector. Set the LoG scale anisotropically, `σ_axis = r_dot/(√3·d_axis)`; measured dot diameter is 5.1 µm ≈ 3 px so this is near the detection floor and subvoxel accuracy *is* the rotation error budget: `σ_θ ≈ σ_d/(R̄√n)`; at `R̄=10 px, σ_d=0.3 px, n=8` → 0.6°.

Binding: `k* = argmin_{k: class=F, ‖x−t_k‖<1.5R̄} |s_k(x) − r_off|`, with `r_off` the median `s` over unambiguous dots as a global nuisance parameter, and `σ_s = √(σ_rough²+σ_d²+σ_fit²)`. Reject at `>3σ_s` → `ORPHAN`; second-best within `2σ_s` → `AMBIGUOUS` (keep and re-resolve after poses converge, do not drop); `k*` is class I → `CROSSTALK`, counted. **Orphan and crosstalk rates are real-data validation statistics.**

Rotation by Kabsch with the **reflection guard**: `H = PᵀWQ`, `U,Σ,Vᵀ = svd(H)`, `D = diag(1,1,sign(det(VUᵀ)))`, `R = VDUᵀ`. Without `D`, noisy near-planar constellations return a reflection (det = −1), which is not a rotation and silently corrupts the track. Uncertainty exactly: `cov(δ) = σ_d²·(Σ_j w_j(‖p_j‖²I − p_jp_jᵀ))⁻¹` — the bracketed matrix is the **inertia tensor of the dot constellation**, and its eigendecomposition is a rigorous per-granule per-frame observability test.

Minimum dot counts fall out of the rank: 1 dot → 1 DOF free; 2 non-collinear → determined but unchecked; 3 → determined + redundancy so outliers are detectable; ≥4 → RANSAC. **Below 2 usable dots, do not fabricate a number** — fall back to body-shape orientation with the `G` branch resolved by temporal continuity, and report a free azimuth as `NaN` with `AZIMUTH_UNOBSERVABLE`. A confident angle here is the worst possible failure because it looks like data.

Correspondence: `scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment` on `C_jm = ‖R̂p_j − q_m‖²`, padded with dummy rows/cols at gate cost so dots may bleach out or appear; 2–3 ICP iterations; run it for **every `g ∈ G`** and keep the lowest rms — *this is the symmetry resolution*; RANSAC (20 iterations for 99% at 60% inliers) when `rms > 3σ_d`. Maintain a per-granule body template with `α=0.2` updates re-projected onto the surface, so `R_template→world(t)` is **absolute** orientation with no accumulated drift, and for F granules the symmetry ambiguity is gone entirely.

### `object_classify.py`
```python
def object_channel_stats(labels, channels, *, erode_voxels=2, fits=None, stats=(...)) -> (K,C,S)
def classify_two_population(stats, *, feature="log_ratio", ch_a, ch_b, depth_correct=True,
                            centroids_um=None, params=None) -> (cls, prob, info)
```
**Erode before measuring** (or measure on `{s_k < −2σ_PSF}` using the fit). PSF bleed across a contact plane is the dominant error in per-object intensity — two touching granules of different colors each acquire ~2σ_PSF of the other's signal and a naive mean pulls both toward the middle. This one line is the difference between 99.5% and 90% accuracy.

Feature `z_k = log((median_A+δ)/(median_B+δ))`: constant fluorescence means per-object intensity = brightness × depth attenuation × bleaching × excitation nonuniformity, and the middle factors are channel-common multiplicative nuisances that a ratio cancels; the log makes what remains two shifted Gaussians of comparable variance. Rule: 2-component 1-D `GaussianMixture` **with a BIC guard over `n_components ∈ {1,2,3}`** — if BIC picks 1, the channels are *not* separating the populations and it must raise rather than bisect noise into two "populations". Depth-correct by regressing `z_k = α + β·z_centroid` and subtracting (default `True` on real data, given the measured monotonic z attenuation). Degraded path without sklearn: the existing `histogram_threshold.suggest_threshold_otsu` on `z`, hard labels, `info["degraded"]=True`.

**Circularity guard**: classify on `z` first, bind dots second, then at most one optional consistency pass. The primary decision must not depend on dots, since dot binding needs the class.

### The synthetic generator: `nodegraph/synth.py`

Placement is mechanical, not stylistic. `scripts/` has **no `__init__.py`** — `_aldvc_validate.py` imports its generator only after a `sys.path.insert`, and `nodegraph/selftest.py` contains **zero** `sys.path` manipulation. Putting the fixture in `scripts/` forces a novel path hack into the most important gate file and makes `python -m nodegraph.selftest` cwd-dependent, which it currently is not. It is also not a `kernels/` module — that directory's stated promise is vendored, individually-documented analysis kernels meant to be ported out.

Guard the "it ships with the package" objection with a **gate instead of a directory**: add a **sixth clause to `test_catalog_import_hygiene`** forbidding any module under `nodegraph/catalog/` from importing `nodegraph.synth`. That is a machine-checked guarantee the fixture never enters a shipped compute path — strictly stronger than the accidental guarantee `scripts/` placement gives. Module scope imports **numpy only**; scipy only inside functions with pure-numpy fallbacks, so the fixture is never the reason a test skips.

Returns `GranuleBed{image (m,t,z,c,y,x) f32, labels_true, dots_true, sizes, metadata, granules, contacts, channel_map, voxel_um}` with `Granule{gid, kind, semi_axes_um, exponents, roughness_seed, centre_um (T,3), quat (T,4), volume_um3, dots_body_um, dot_ids}`. Add a **`dims=2` mode** producing superellipse beds with an in-plane angle, and a **polydisperse** option matching the measured 4× size range — both required by the confirmed decisions.

**Contact is a property of the construction, not a hope about the RNG**: (1) Bridson dart-throwing for bulk placement (lift `_aldvc_synth.poisson_disc_3d`'s approach, O(n)); (2) exact tangency by radius assignment — set `r_i = (d_nn/2)(1+gap)`, so both members of a mutual-nearest-neighbour pair get `d_nn/2` and their surfaces meet exactly at the midpoint; (3) `n_forced_contacts` hand-placed pairs at overlap ∈ {0, 0.5, 1.0} voxels whose answer is hand-computable and RNG-independent. And a triaxial superellipsoid is invariant under `{I,Rx(π),Ry(π),Rz(π)}`, so a **correct** fit may return any of 4 quaternions — hence `rot_error_deg` quotients by the group. A naive quaternion assertion fails ~75% of the time on correct code.

## Fourth deliverable: void space, compaction, and F/I clustering

Beyond per-granule volumes, pose, and the confidence field, there is a fourth output: **segment the space between the granules** and characterize it, to study **clustering and compaction via void-space distributions**, with void space **attributed to the F and I phases**. So the segmentation is three-phase — F solid, I solid, void.

This makes the tessellation question load-bearing rather than cosmetic: for non-overlapping bodies, nearest-surface and the power diagram are *both exactly correct on the solids*, so they differ **only in how they divide background** — which is precisely the void measurement. A tessellation investigation was run (theory, a real-data measurement, an adversarial audit of that measurement, and a repo survey). Its results are below. **The void-space design workflow was lost to the session limit, so the void metrology and its node design remain the one genuinely open piece** — see "Still open" at the end of this section.

### Established (survived audit, with numbers)

**1. Nearest-surface assignment *is* a tessellation** — the Set Voronoi / navigational tessellation in its exact, sampling-free form. There is a clean theorem behind it:

> For closed bodies `B₁…B_K` with pairwise disjoint interiors and `x ∈ int B_i \ ⋃_{j≠i} B_j`: `s_i(x) < 0 ≤ s_j(x)`, hence `argmin_k s_k(x) = i`. The identical argument holds for `pow_i` on balls.

Corollaries: each body lies entirely inside its own cell in **both** diagrams, so `φ_local ≤ 1` by construction; the two diagrams **agree on every voxel inside a body**; and because the argument uses no sphericity or convexity, the signed-distance form **survives superellipsoids and even concave shapes**. Measured: power vs signed-distance disagree on **9.4% of all space but 0.000% inside the solid** — 100% of the disagreement is in the empty medium (13.0% of void voxels). This confirms the analytical prediction exactly.

**2. Ordinary Voronoi on centroids is disqualified.** Against synthetic ground truth where pixel ownership is exactly known (450 non-overlapping tangent discs at the measured size distribution), contested-domain pixel accuracy: watershed **99.99%**, nearest-surface **99.96%**, power **99.94%**, **ordinary Voronoi 95.39%** — the sole loser, by 4.6 points. At 15% overlap: Voronoi 90.79% vs ~96% for the rest. The error is **systematic against large granules** (the larger disc penetrates its own bisector by up to 3.7× the *smaller* disc's radius; at φ=0.416 the largest 20% of discs lose 10.2% of their area on average, max 44.6%). On real data the *effect size* is threshold-dependent (at a Triangle threshold every method falls within one voxel), but the *direction* holds in 11/11 field/threshold configurations, paired Wilcoxon **p=0.0010**.

**3. The power diagram is computable exactly via a kD-tree lift — and this settles a contradiction between two agents.** One agent asserted "a KD-tree cannot answer a power query directly"; that is **false**, and the other agent both derived and numerically verified the standard lifting trick. With `H = max_i R_i`, lift sites to `(c_i, √(H² − R_i²))` and queries to `(x, 0)`:

```
|(x,0) − (c_i, √(H²−R_i²))|²  =  |x−c_i|² + H² − R_i²  =  pow_i(x) + H²
```

`H²` is site-independent, so the argmin is **identical**. Verified at **0 mismatches out of 1,048,576 pixels** against brute-force power argmin, at **0.06 s vs 10.71 s — a 169× speedup** (1024², K=453). In 3D (7×1024×1024 = 7.34 M voxels, 4D lift): 0.33 s. The trick is `d → d+1` for any `d`, so it is dimension-generic for free.

**4. Seeding, not partitioning, is the bottleneck — and this is the most consequential finding.** On a haze-bridged dense synthetic, h-maxima seeding **missed 205 of 400 discs (51%)**, while all six partition rules differed by **≤7% of contested pixels**. On real data, foreground fragments into **1002 connected components of which 914 contain zero seeds** (40% of foreground is seedless debris), and 30–33% of components hold ≥2 seeds. Radius estimation is *not* the problem (§ size correction above); **seed count is.** This plan currently lavishes effort on the partition — probability field, competitive growth, ICM, surface energy, EM relaxation — and treats seeding as a one-line EDT h-maxima step. **That is backwards and the increment order below is revised accordingly.**

**5. Watershed on `Λ = min_k s_k` reproduces the tessellation in the median but fails exactly where it matters.** Median displacement **0.37 px**, but **max 15.46 px, with 17% of pairs exceeding 1 px** — and **one component carries 51% of all disagreement, the top three carry 82%**, and those are the 6-, 7- and 9-seed components, i.e. the multi-contact clusters the whole exercise exists to resolve. The number also **halves with `connectivity=1` vs `2`**, an unreported parameter. So "competitive growth already *is* the tessellation" is true on average and false at the hard cases; flooding a landscape is not `argmin_k s_k` on a masked concave domain (disconnected cells, flood-order ties).

**6. A correction to this plan's stated identity.** `argmax_k p_k = argmin_k s_k` holds **only** when the intensity and log-prior terms are equal across `k` (same colour class, equal `π_k`). In general the argmax minimizes `s_k + λ_I(…)ℓ − ℓ log π_k`. Because `|∇s| = 1` for a true SDF, an energy imbalance of `ΔE` µm displaces the boundary by exactly `ΔE` µm — so **any intensity or prior term exceeding 1.72 µm moves the boundary more than one voxel. That is the calibration for `λ_I`.** Also "decision surface at the median plane" is true only for `R₁ = R₂`; in general it is a hyperboloid (`b² = R₁R₂` at tangency). The estimator claim survives because both forms are exact on the solid.

**7. The background class is what converts a tessellation back into a segmentation.** At φ≈0.28, **72% of the plane is void**, and a tessellation labels every bit of it. `E_∅ = m₀` is therefore load-bearing, not decoration — keep it.

**8. Border truncation silently breaks the theorems.** Clipping cells at the frame produced `φ_i` up to **1.25** (power) and **1.69** (Voronoi) — a spurious violation of `φ_i ≤ 1` caused purely by truncation. Every `φ_i` / Euler / residual statistic must be **restricted to cells whose closure does not touch the frame**. `scipy.spatial.Voronoi` returns `-1` vertices for unbounded cells and the repo's kernel skips them → NaN volume, silently dropping all border granules from statistics.

**9. `φ_local` alone is not a merge/split discriminator — it is merge-invariant.** Merging two radius-`R` discs into one radius-`√2 R` fit leaves `φ = |B|/|cell|` unchanged. What works is the **unexplained-foreground residual**:

```
η_i = |{v ∈ cell_i : foreground}| / |cell_i|      (cell fill)
φ_i = |B_i| / |cell_i|                            (own occupancy)
r_i = η_i − φ_i  ∈ [−1, 1]
```

`r_i = 0` for a correct segmentation; a **missing seed drives `r_i` up by exactly the missing object's area / |cell_i|** and, crucially, **localizes the error to one cell**. Given finding 4, this is the single most valuable diagnostic in the whole plan. A voxel field cannot supply it — you need the cells.

**10. Coordination number is unusable on this data, twice over.** In 2D, mean cell degree is pinned to ~6 by Euler (`E = 3F − 6`; measured 1338 edges vs 1356 predicted, mean 5.91), so **it carries zero packing information**. And the measured `Z̄` is method-dependent (1.28–1.55 on fixed seeds) and structurally forced (12 of 22 contested components hold exactly 2 seeds, forcing `Z=1`). The defensible statement is "**this acquisition cannot measure in-plane coordination**", not "this bed is not jammed". No `Z`-based prior and no `Z`-based validation. Only the *distribution* of edges per cell is potentially informative.

### The recommended role for tessellation

Ranked by value per unit of work:

1. **The `r_i` residual as a segmentation error detector** (finding 9) — attacks the actual bottleneck, localizes errors to a cell, cheap.
2. **Exact, parameter-free adjacency for the Gibbs prior and the merge/split moves.** The lifted hull gives 1338 truly-adjacent pairs in 2.4 ms; `cKDTree.query_pairs` gives 2789 — a **2.08× over-generation** — and needs a cutoff keyed to `R_max`. The power adjacency is exactly the set of pairs that *can* share a boundary, which is the correct support for a pair potential, and `adj_dist_um` disappears.
3. **Void attribution and analytic cell areas** (the fourth deliverable). Polygonal cells remove voxel quantisation, which matters: a 9 µm-radius granule is 86 px with a 33 px perimeter, so a **half-voxel** surface-placement bias costs ~19% of its area.
4. **A 169× cheaper exact implementation** of the isotropic case (finding 3) — implementation economy, not accuracy.
5. **Topological consistency checks** (Euler `V/E/F`) that a voxel field cannot provide at all.

**Ruled out:** tessellation does *not* replace competitive growth or rescue the contacts. Against ground truth, watershed (0.01% error) and nearest-surface (0.04%) are a genuine tie; the two proxies that appeared to favour the tessellation both failed the audit (one was circular — it used the watershed objective as the referee for watershed-vs-tessellation).

**Critical scope limit:** all of this is established **for discs/spheres only**. For superellipses with an in-plane angle — the actual confirmed scope — the nearest-surface partition is a genuine *anisotropic* Set-Voronoi with **no exact power-diagram equivalent**; the radical-axis flatness that makes power ≈ nearest-surface collapses. So the power diagram is a fast **initializer, adjacency source, and void-attribution tessellation**, while the SDF field remains the operative machinery for the fitted superquadrics. If combinatorics are later wanted for anisotropic shapes, the principled route is an anisotropic power diagram (cost `‖x−c_i‖²_{A_i} − w_i`, curved faces, volumes matched by semi-discrete optimal transport).

### Plan delta

- **`analysis.voronoi` gains a weighted metric.** New `Mode("metric", ["euclidean", "power", "nearest_surface"], default="euclidean")` — `euclidean` preserves today's behaviour so the baseline default stays semantically identical. The per-seed radius arrives as a **column, not a scalar** (a scalar collapses power back to Euclidean): a new `InString("radius_col", …, default="")` with **no `layer_in`**, naming a column of the Point layer already chosen by `points` — exactly the precedent of `analysis.tessellate`'s `cluster` socket (`tessellate.py:204-211`). Implement the argmin by the kD-tree lift (finding 3), not a candidate-rerank.
  **Correctness verified on the existing blocking scheme:** arena grouping survives (it restricts the *candidate set*, which is metric-agnostic) and the 2¹⁸-voxel block survives (it chunks *query points*, not sites). The only invalid Euclidean-derived prune is **`distance_upper_bound`** — power distance is not a distance and is negative inside a sphere. Fix by relocating the gate: compute the argmin unpruned, then gate `|x − c_winner| > upper → unclaimed`, which preserves the socket's documented meaning exactly.
- **The adjacency relation lives as a second `StructureTable` on `Domain.LABEL`** under `f"{name}_contacts"`, modelled byte-for-byte on `TrackMembership.to_table()`: columns `a, b` (canonical `a < b`, one row per unordered pair), `contact_area_um2`, `m, t, c`, announced via `extra_layers`. **No new domain.** The precedent is decisive — `Domain.TRACK`'s membership table already ships with no `id`/`z`/`y`/`x` columns, there is no runtime `COORD_COLUMNS` validator, and a pair table is therefore already a legal, tested shape that inherits the spreadsheet, content hash, Arrow export and `z_kind` provenance for free. A `Domain.CONTACT` would additionally require a `bridges.py` entry, and that is **fatal by construction**: every existing bridge keys on a *single* id, so `CONTACT → LABEL` is ambiguous (does object `a` take row `(a,b)` or `(b,a)`, summed, maxed?) — the reduction must be *chosen*, which makes it a node parameter, not a bridge. Plus per-object summary columns (`n_contacts`, `contact_area_um2`, `coordination`) on the existing LABEL table via `with_layer`, the `analysis.object_metrics` pattern.
- **Add `r_i` (finding 9) as LABEL columns** `cell_fill`, `cell_occupancy`, `cell_residual` alongside `cell_area_um2` / `free_area_um2` / `local_packing_fraction`, all computed by whichever node produced the cells, and all **excluded for frame-touching cells** (finding 8).
- **The dead merge loop: keep the scaffold, kill the criterion.** The machinery is right-shaped (iterative RAG, union-find, rebuild-and-restart, fixpoint driver) but the objective `|d_i − d_j|/max(d_i,d_j)` with `d = n_points/volume` **compares sampling artefacts, not evidence** — the same granule sampled at two surface densities scores as two objects. It also only ever *merges*, while our dominant error is under-segmentation. Revive the scaffold only, behind pluggable `score(pair)` / `accept(score)`, with the default score a signed **`ΔE = E(merged) − E(i) − E(j)`** including the removed hard-core pair term and the removed `γ̃·A_ij` boundary area. Surfaced behaviour is then "merge adjacent regions whose ΔE < 0" — charter-general, with the energy supplied by the caller. If `_adjacency_alpha` is swapped for the power adjacency, `adj_dist_um` disappears.

### Tests this adds

- **Pin the weighting so it cannot silently regress to ordinary Voronoi**: on a synthetic polydisperse packing with known ownership, `metric=power` achieves ≥99.9% contested-pixel accuracy while `metric=euclidean` scores ≤96% — assert *both bounds*, so a broken weight column fails rather than passing quietly.
- **Pin the lift's exactness**: `metric=power` via the lift must equal brute-force power argmin at **0 mismatched pixels**, not "approximately".
- `φ_i ≤ 1` for every non-frame-touching cell under `power` and `nearest_surface`; assert the frame-touching exclusion is what makes it true (removing the exclusion must produce `φ_i > 1`).
- `r_i ≈ 0` on a correctly-seeded synthetic bed, and `r_i` rising by the missing object's area fraction when one seed is deliberately deleted — with the error localized to the correct cell.
- Adjacency table: `a < b` canonical, no duplicate pairs, and `n_contacts` on the LABEL table equal to each object's degree in the pair table.
- **Do not** assert any coordination number or jamming property (finding 10).

### Still open — the void workflow was lost

The void-space metrology and node design produced no output. Outstanding before the fourth deliverable can be built:

- **Stereology.** Area fraction is an unbiased estimator of volume fraction (Delesse/Rosiwal), so **bulk porosity should be recoverable**; but a section's size distribution is a size-biased, shrunken view of the 3D distribution (Wicksell's corpuscle problem), so pore-size and cell-volume distributions are **biased and must not be reported as 3D**. Note the measured in-plane φ ≈ 0.28 is far below random loose packing (~0.55) and 2D MRJ discs (~0.83), and it falls to 0.014 by z=6 — which is **attenuation, not physics**. So `φ_3D` is not measurable from this acquisition either; do not hard-code it.
- **Clustering statistics** and their false-positive control. The primary test should be a **join-count permutation** on the contact network (relabel species holding positions *and* the network fixed), size-stratified if F and I differ in size — a naive `g(r)` on unequal-sized particles shows structure from excluded volume alone. Any recommended statistic must be validated to report *non*-significance on a randomly relabelled packing.
- **Void attribution scheme.** Leading candidate needs no new machinery: sum `p_k(v)` over granules of phase F to get `P_F(v)`, with `E_∅` carrying unattributable bulk void. Compare against hard power-cell membership and a distance-band decomposition before building anything.
- **Where a curve-shaped output lives** (`g(r)`, `K(r)`) — no current domain holds a function of `r`; a structure table whose rows are `r`-bins is the likely answer but was not designed.
- **Excluding void outside the bed** (above the top layer, at the frame) so it does not contaminate porosity.

## 0. Architecture in one paragraph

CellSAM answers *"granule or background"*. The Euclidean distance transform answers *"how many granules and where are their centers"*. ## 1. `nodegraph/kernels/superellipsoid.py`

### 1.1 Parameterization

Barr superellipsoid, body frame, semi-axes `(a,b,c)`, shape exponents `(ε₁,ε₂)`:

```
S = (|x|/a)^(2/ε₂) + (|y|/b)^(2/ε₂)
F(x,y,z) = S^(ε₂/ε₁) + (|z|/c)^(2/ε₁)
```

`F < 1` inside, `= 1` on surface, `> 1` outside. `ε₁` shapes the z-profile, `ε₂` the cross-section. `ε=1` → ellipsoid; `ε→0` → box; `ε→2` → octahedron.

**The single most useful property:** `F` is positively homogeneous of degree `2/ε₁`:

```
F(λu) = λ^(2/ε₁) F(u)     for λ > 0
```

Hence the point on the surface along the ray `Ou` is `βu` with `β = F(u)^(−ε₁/2)`, so the **signed radial distance** in the body frame is

```
r(u) = ‖u‖ · ( 1 − F(u)^(−ε₁/2) )                       (µm, + outside)
```

which equivalently equals `‖u‖ − ρ(û)` where `ρ(û) = F(û)^(−ε₁/2)` is the surface radius in direction `û`. This is well-behaved everywhere including `u = 0` (where it → `−ρ(û)` for any `û`; guard `‖u‖ < 1e-9` by returning `−min(a,b,c)`). It is **exact** for spheres, and for a star-shaped surface it satisfies `|d_Euclid| ≤ |r|` with ratio bounded by `1/cos θ` (θ = angle between the ray and the surface normal); for `ε ≤ 1.4` and axis ratio ≤ 2.5 the overshoot is < 12%. Same sign, same zero set, monotone in the same direction → level-set topology of the probability field is unaffected.

With pose: `u = Rᵀ(x − t)`, and

```
s(x; θ) = r(Rᵀ(x − t)),        θ = (t∈ℝ³, δ∈ℝ³, a, b, c, ε₁, ε₂)     — 11 params
```

### 1.2 Which residual, and why not `(F−1)`

`F − 1` is **degree-`2/ε₁` homogeneous**, so with `ε₁ ∈ [0.2, 2]` the exponent spans `[1, 10]`. A fixed tolerance on `|F−1|` corresponds to a metric error that varies by orders of magnitude with `ε₁`, the Jacobian columns for `(a,b,c)` and `(ε₁,ε₂)` differ in scale by `10^k`, and Levenberg–Marquardt trades shape against scale to game the exponent rather than fit the surface. The classic Solina–Bajcsy fix `√(abc)·(F^{ε₁} − 1)` fixes the volume-runaway but is still not in length units.

**Use `s(x;θ)` directly.** It is degree-1 homogeneous, in µm, has a physical scale (compare to `σ_PSF`), and lets `loss='soft_l1'` with `f_scale = 2σ_rough` mean exactly "treat deviations larger than twice the roughness amplitude as outliers". Taubin's gradient-corrected form `(F−1)/‖∇F‖` is the alternative; reject it because `‖∇F‖` → 0 at axis crossings when `ε > 1` and → ∞ when `ε < 1`, i.e. it is worst precisely at the corners that carry the shape information. Radial is exact along rays and needs one `F` evaluation.

### 1.3 Residual vector

Stack, for a weighted point set `{x_i, w_i}` (surface-band voxels, or all voxels weighted by `p_k(v)`):

```
ρ_data[i]   = √w_i · s(x_i; θ)                                        (N rows)
ρ_size      = w_s · ( (abc)^(1/3) − R̄ ) / σ_R                          (1 row)
ρ_aspect[m] = w_ar · softplus( log(A_m) − log τ_ar ) ,  m = 1..3        (3 rows)
              A = (a/b, b/c, a/c) taken as max(·, 1/·)
ρ_eps[j]    = w_ε · (ε_j − 1) / σ_ε ,  j = 1,2                          (2 rows)
```

- `ρ_size` is the known-size prior. Recommended `σ_R = 0.1 R̄`, `w_s = √N/3` so the prior has the weight of ~N/9 points.
- `ρ_aspect` is the **high-aspect-ratio regularizer the user asked for, at the fit level**: `softplus(x) = log(1+e^x)`, so it is ≈0 for axis ratios below `τ_ar = 2.5` and grows linearly beyond. Smooth (LM-friendly), one-sided.
- `ρ_eps` shrinks toward the ellipsoid; with `σ_ε = 0.5` it only bites when the data cannot support a non-unit exponent. This is the cure for exponent/roughness confounding (§9.6).
- Bounds via `least_squares(bounds=...)`: `ε ∈ [0.25, 1.8]`, `a,b,c ∈ [0.5R̄, 2R̄]`, `‖δ‖ ≤ 0.6` rad per outer step.

### 1.4 Initialization from image moments

Weighted moments in µm, `W = Σw_i`:

```
t₀ = Σ w_i x_i / W
C  = Σ w_i (x_i−t₀)(x_i−t₀)ᵀ / W
λ₁≥λ₂≥λ₃, E = eigh(C)   →  R₀ = [e₁ e₂ e₃], columns reordered descending
e₃ ← e₁ × e₂            →  guarantees det R₀ = +1
```

Semi-axes: for a **uniform solid** ellipsoid `λ₁ = a²/5`, so naively `a = √(5λ₁)`. But at a contact the mask is truncated and `λ` is biased low. Use **eigenvalue ratios for shape, the known size for scale**:

```
g = (λ₁λ₂λ₃)^(1/6)                       (geometric-mean length scale)
a₀ = R̄ · √(λ₁)/g ,  b₀ = R̄ · √(λ₂)/g ,  c₀ = R̄ · √(λ₃)/g
```

so `(a₀b₀c₀)^(1/3) = R̄` exactly, and only the aspect ratios come from the (possibly truncated) mask. For general `ε` the coefficient `1/5` becomes `κ(ε₁,ε₂) = V⁻¹∫(x/a)²dV`; **do not hand-derive it** — tabulate `κ` on a 9×9 `(ε₁,ε₂)` grid by quadrature once at import (cached module constant, ~µs bilinear lookup) and divide. Sanity anchor: the exact volume is

```
V(a,b,c,ε₁,ε₂) = 2abc·ε₁·ε₂·B(ε₁/2+1, ε₁)·B(ε₂/2, ε₂/2)
```
which at `ε₁=ε₂=1` gives `2abc·(2/3)·π = (4/3)πabc` ✔ — use that identity as a unit test of the Beta-function code.

### 1.5 Exponent non-convexity

`ε` is where the local minima are; the other 9 params are well-behaved for fixed `ε`. **Nested grid-then-refine:**

1. Coarse grid `ε₁,ε₂ ∈ {0.4, 0.7, 1.0, 1.4}` (16 combos). For each, `least_squares` over the 9 pose+size params, `max_nfev = 25`.
2. Take the argmin cost; run a full 11-param `least_squares` from it, `max_nfev = 60`.
3. If the refined `ε` lands on a bound, or if cost₂/cost₁ > 0.95 (grid didn't help), report `eps_confident = False`.

### 1.6 Analytic Jacobian — required, not optional

Finite differences cost `(n+1)` residual evaluations per Jacobian; with the grid that is ~950 s for K=2200 granules. The analytic Jacobian is a 10× win and brings it to ~10 s. Let `G = F^(−ε₁/2)`, `r = ‖u‖(1−G)`, `A = (|x|/a)^(2/ε₂)`, `B = (|y|/b)^(2/ε₂)`, `Z = (|z|/c)^(2/ε₁)`, `S = A+B`, `P = S^(ε₂/ε₁)`.

```
∂F/∂x = (2/ε₁)·(A/x)·P/S            (and the y analogue with B)
∂F/∂z = (2/ε₁)·(Z/z)
∂F/∂a = −(2/(ε₁ a))·A·P/S
∂F/∂b = −(2/(ε₁ b))·B·P/S
∂F/∂c = −(2/(ε₁ c))·Z
∂F/∂ε₁ = −(ε₂/ε₁²)·P·ln S  −  (2/ε₁²)·Z·ln(|z|/c)
∂F/∂ε₂ = (1/ε₁)·P·ln S  −  (2/(ε₁ ε₂))·P·(A ln(|x|/a) + B ln(|y|/b))/S
∂r/∂u  = (1−G)·û + ‖u‖·(ε₁/2)·G·F⁻¹·∇_u F
∂r/∂t  = −Rᵀᵀ (∂r/∂u) = −R (∂r/∂u)          [u = Rᵀ(x−t)]
∂r/∂δ  = −u × (∂r/∂u)                        [R = R_ref·exp([δ]×), evaluated at δ=0]
∂r/∂q  = −‖u‖·∂G/∂q  for q ∈ {a,b,c,ε₂},  ∂G/∂q = −(ε₁/2)G F⁻¹ ∂F/∂q
∂r/∂ε₁ = −‖u‖·G·( −½ln F − (ε₁/2)F⁻¹∂F/∂ε₁ )
```

Guards: clamp `|x| ≥ 1e-9a` etc. before the `A/x` division; when `A+B = 0` (on the z-axis) set the `x,y` gradient terms to 0. **Ship a gradcheck unit test**: max relative error vs 2-point finite differences < 1e-5 over 500 random `(θ, x)` — the `∂r/∂δ` sign in particular must be verified numerically, not by inspection.

### 1.7 Roughness as a residual statistic, not a model

After the fit, the signed residuals `d_i = s(x_i; θ̂)` **are** the roughness field sampled at surface voxels. Report:

```
σ_rough = 1.4826 · median(|d_i − median d|)                (robust amplitude)
```

and, because `synth.py` generates **band-limited spherical roughness**, fit real spherical harmonics on the body-frame directions `û_i`:

```
d_i ≈ Σ_{l=0..L} Σ_{m=−l..l} c_lm Y_lm(û_i),     L = 6  (49 columns)
c = (YᵀWY + λI)⁻¹ YᵀW d,   λ = 1e-3·tr(YᵀWY)/49
P_l = Σ_m c_lm²
```

`lstsq` on 2000×49 is microseconds. Truncated masks (contact flattening) leave a missing cap → ill-conditioning; if `cond(YᵀWY) > 1e6`, report `σ_rough` only and set `P_l = NaN`. This makes roughness a *free byproduct* — no roughness parameters in the fit, and `P_l` is directly comparable to synth ground truth.

**Contact-flattening bias, and the fix using existing code.** Surface voxels in a contact region have systematically negative residuals (the mask is cut by the median plane, not by the granule surface), which biases `a,b,c` small and `σ_rough` large. Fix: reuse `granule_boundary.extract_boundary_bands` to get, per granule, the voxels whose outward band hits a *different* label — that is the contact set. Down-weight those points `w_i ← 0.1` in the fit and exclude them from the roughness statistics. This is the single highest-value reuse in the design.

### 1.9 Complexity / memory / deps / failure

- Fit: 16 grid × 25 iters × 1 Jacobian eval (analytic) × `N_s` points, `N_s` sub-sampled to 500 surface voxels → **~5 ms/granule**, K=2200 → ~11 s. Embarrassingly parallel over granules; accept `progress_cb` like `cellsam_segment` does.
- `signed_radial_grid` memory: one granule bbox, `(1.4·2a/dx)³` ≈ 70³ f32 = 1.4 MB. Never allocate `K × Z·Y·X`.
- Deps: numpy, `scipy.optimize.least_squares`, `scipy.special.beta`, `scipy.linalg.eigh`. **No sklearn, no torch.** Degraded path: if `least_squares` fails to converge (`status ≤ 0`), fall back to `moment_init` (pure closed-form ellipsoid) and set `flags |= FIT_FAILED`. ## 2. `nodegraph/kernels/so3.py`

### 2.1 Fit parameterization: local rotation-vector increment

Recommend **`R = R_ref · exp([δ]×)`, fitting the 3-vector `δ` and retracting `R_ref ← R_ref exp([δ]×)`, `δ ← 0` between outer iterations.**

- Euler angles: 3 params but the Jacobian is rank-deficient at ±90° (gimbal lock) and `least_squares` bounds interact badly with angle wrapping. Reject.
- Unit quaternion as 4 free params: the norm constraint makes `JᵀJ` singular along the radial direction; you must either normalize inside the residual (which injects a null direction LM handles poorly) or add a Lagrange row. Reject for the optimizer.
- Rotation matrix, 9 params + 6 constraints: reject.
- Local `so(3)` increment: exactly 3 DOF, no singularity for `‖δ‖ < π`, best-conditioned Jacobian at `δ = 0` (which is where you always are, by construction). This is the standard local-parameterization/retraction trick. **Recommend.**

**Store** as a quaternion (`w ≥ 0` canonical) plus the cached `R`, because the symmetry quotient, averaging, and interpolation all live naturally in quaternion space.

```
θ = ‖δ‖,  K = [δ]×/θ
exp([δ]×) = I + sin θ · K + (1 − cos θ) K²          (Taylor for θ < 1e-8: I + [δ]× + ½[δ]×²)
log(R): θ = arccos(clip((tr R − 1)/2, −1, 1)),  δ = θ/(2 sin θ) · (R − Rᵀ)_vee
        near θ=π use the eigenvector of R for eigenvalue +1, sign-fixed by (R−Rᵀ)
```

### 2.2 Symmetry groups — a first-class API

A triaxial superellipsoid with `a≠b≠c` is invariant under `D₂ = {I, Rx(π), Ry(π), Rz(π)}`, order 4. **But the group depends on the fitted shape**, and getting this wrong is the difference between a working tracker and garbage:

| condition | rotation symmetry group | order | unobservable DOF |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a≠b≠c` | `D₂` | 4 | 0 (4 discrete branches) |
| `a≈b≠c`, `ε₂ ≠ 1` | `D₄` (adds `Rz(±π/2)`) | 8 | 0 (8 branches) |
| `a≈b≠c`, `ε₂ = 1` | continuous `SO(2)_z` | ∞ | 1 (azimuth) |
| `a≈b≈c`, `ε₁=ε₂` | octahedral `O` | 24 | 0 |
| `a≈b≈c`, `ε₁=ε₂=1` | `SO(3)` | ∞ | 3 |

```python
def symmetry_group(fit, *, tol_axis=0.03, tol_eps=0.05) -> SymmetryGroup
@dataclass SymmetryGroup: kind: str; mats: np.ndarray  # (G,3,3); free_axis: np.ndarray|None; n_free: int
def geodesic_angle(R1, R2) -> float                       # arccos(clip((tr(R1ᵀR2)−1)/2,−1,1))
def reduced_angle(R1, R2, group) -> float                 # min over g of geodesic_angle(R1, R2 @ g)
def align_to_reference(R, R_ref, group) -> (R_aligned, g_index)
def angular_velocity(quats, dt, group) -> (omega (T-1,3), alpha (T-2,3))
def kabsch(P, Q, w=None) -> (R, rms, cov_delta)
def quat_from_matrix(R) / matrix_from_quat(q) / quat_mul / quat_canonical
```

**Every rotation error metric in this project must be `reduced_angle`, never `geodesic_angle`.** And `tol_axis` must not be a magic constant — set it from the fit's own uncertainty: `tol_axis = 3·σ_a/a` with `σ_a` from `cov_pose`. Additionally declare an axis unobservable when the largest eigenvalue of the `δδ` block of `cov_pose` exceeds `(10°)²`. That is a data-driven degeneracy test, which is strictly better than thresholding `|a−b|`. Run both; OR them.

### 2.3 Resolving the ambiguity

**F granules (have dots): fully resolved, absolutely.** The dot constellation is generically invariant under no `g ∈ G`. Pick

```
g* = argmin_{g∈G}  Σ_j w_j ‖ R̂ g p_j − (x_j − t) ‖²
```
This eliminates the branch entirely and gives absolute orientation relative to a fixed body template — not just increments (§6).

**I granules (no dots): irreducible.** Absolute orientation is *not identifiable*; only the coset `R·G ∈ SO(3)/G` is. Be explicit in the output: publish `quat` plus `symmetry_order`, and document that comparisons must use `reduced_angle`. Cumulative rotation from `t=0` is still recoverable by branch continuation (choose per frame the `g` minimizing distance to `t−1`) **provided the per-frame rotation is below half the minimum symmetry angle**:

```
Nyquist condition:   |ω| Δt  <  θ_min(G)/2 ,   θ_min(D₂) = 90° → 45°,  θ_min(D₄) = 45° → 22.5°
```

Above that, aliasing is mathematically irrecoverable from body shape alone. This is a hard limit, must be surfaced as a computed per-granule flag (`ALIASING_RISK` when the observed `|ω|Δt > 0.7·θ_min/2`), and must be an explicit swept test in validation (§10).

### 2.4 Per-frame independent fit vs temporal propagation

**Recommend: independent per-frame fit, warm-started, followed by branch resolution, followed by optional manifold smoothing.**

1. Link centroids over time first with the existing `kernels/track_objects.py` (`centroid` or `overlap` method). Note its topology descriptors build a rotation-**invariant** frame and therefore must not be used for orientation — only for identity linking.
2. Fit frame `t` with `R_ref = R̂_{t−1}` and `t_ref` from the track (warm start ⇒ ~3× fewer LM iterations, and `δ` stays small so the local parameterization is valid).
3. Resolve `g_t` by `align_to_reference(R̃_t, R̂_{t−1}, G)`; dots override when `n_dots ≥ 2`.
4. Smooth on the manifold only if requested: `R̂_t ← R̂_t exp(½·log(R̂_{t−1}ᵀ R̂_t) + ½·log(R̂_{t+1}ᵀR̂_t))`-style geodesic filter, or a 5-tap SG filter in the tangent at `R̂_t`.

Rationale: pure propagation (register `t` against `t−1` only) accumulates drift and cannot recover from a single bad frame; pure independent fitting flips between symmetry branches. The hybrid keeps an auditable per-frame residual (`rms_um`) that lets you reject a frame (`rms > 3× median` → interpolate pose geodesically from neighbours, flag `INTERPOLATED`).

## 3. `nodegraph/kernels/label_stack_link.py` — seeds, and a blunt statement about CellSAM

### 3.1 The reformulation

`cellsam_segment.segment_plane` is **strictly 2D and returns labels only** — `mask_threshold` consumes the decoder logits internally, so there is no confidence to propagate. Worse: CellSAM is trained on cells with membranes/boundaries. This scene is a bed of *filled, constant-intensity* solids with **no boundary signal at contacts**. Expect systematic under-segmentation (merged contacting granules) precisely in the hard case.

**Recommended architecture — use each tool for what it is actually good at:**

- **CellSAM → foreground.** `mask = relabel_contiguous(segment_plane(...)) > 0`, unioned over z. Its strength is "granule vs background", which is real and non-trivial with uneven illumination and depth attenuation. Do **not** trust its instance boundaries.
- **EDT h-maxima → instance count and seeds.** For a jammed bed of known-size convex solids, the interior maxima of the distance transform *are* the centroids, and contacts create saddles exactly at the contact planes. This is five lines and will very likely beat CellSAM at instance separation here:

```
D   = distance_transform_edt(mask, sampling=(dz,dy,dx))          # µm
Dh  = h_maxima(D, h)                     h = 0.25 R̄
seeds = label(Dh, structure=ones(3,3,3))
reject seed if D[seed] < 0.55 R̄            (too shallow to be a granule core)
merge seed pairs closer than r_hard         (§4.3, the Gibbs hard core)
```

- **Reconcile:** run EDT h-maxima *inside* each CellSAM-linked component. If a component contains `n > 1` seeds, it is a CellSAM merge and the seeds split it. If a component contains 0 seeds and its volume `< 0.3 V̄`, it is debris — drop it.
- **Count sanity gate:** the expected count from geometry is `K̂_exp ≈ φ·V_fg/V̄` with packing fraction `φ ∈ [0.55, 0.68]`. If `|K̂ − K̂_exp|/K̂_exp > 0.30`, **raise** — a 30% count error means the segmentation is fundamentally wrong and every downstream statistic is meaningless. This is the one place a hard raise is justified.

### 3.2 2D→3D linking

Per adjacent z-pair, cost `C_{ij} = 1 − IoU(L_z=i, L_{z+1}=j)`, `scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment` with a gate `IoU ≥ 0.3`, then union-find over accepted pairs. Hungarian, not greedy: greedy IoU chaining creates chains that hop between granules in a dense bed. Many-to-one merges are *allowed* here (the EDT split fixes them afterwards).

```python
def link_labels_z(labels_zhw, *, iou_min=0.3, min_planes=3) -> (np.ndarray int32, dict)
def edt_seeds(mask_zhw, voxel_size_um, *, r_known_um, h_frac=0.25,
              min_depth_frac=0.55, r_hard_um=None) -> (seed_labels int32, centroids_um (K,3), dict)
def reconcile_seeds(components_int32, seed_labels, *, min_vol_frac=0.3) -> (labels int32, dict)
```

Deps: scipy.ndimage (`distance_transform_edt`, `label`), skimage.morphology (`h_maxima`) — degraded path if skimage absent: `h_maxima` is reconstructible as `D − reconstruction(D − h, D)`, or use `maximum_filter(D, size=2R̄/d) == D` with NMS. Complexity: one EDT over the foreground, `O(N)` with the scipy separable algorithm; 67 M voxels ≈ 8 s, one f64 copy = 537 MB → cast to f32 immediately, or run per-slab with 2R̄ overlap.

## 4. `nodegraph/kernels/granule_probability.py`

### 4.1 What the probability IS

A **Gibbs (Boltzmann) posterior of a generative mixture over voxels**, whose energy is dominated by the geometric signed distance. Written once, in µm, with a single temperature outside:

```
E_k(v) = s_k(v)  +  λ_I · ½(I(v) − µ_{c(k)})ᵀΣ_c⁻¹(I(v) − µ_{c(k)})·ℓ  −  ℓ·log π_k
E_∅(v) = m₀                                        (outlier/background class, m₀ ≈ 1.5 σ_PSF)

p_k(v) = exp(−E_k(v)/T) / ( exp(−E_∅(v)/T) + Σ_{j∈cand(v)} exp(−E_j(v)/T) )
```

`ℓ` is a length scale converting the dimensionless intensity term to µm (set `ℓ = σ_PSF`). `T` is in **µm** and has a physical meaning: the surface localization uncertainty, `T ≈ σ_PSF + σ_rough`.

Why this and not something else:

1. It **is** the exact posterior of a Gibbs model whose energy is the geometric distance — not a heuristic squashing function. `T` is an interpretable nuisance parameter that can be *calibrated* (§10).
2. `argmax_k p_k = argmin_k s_k` = **nearest-surface assignment**, which is the provably correct estimator for two identical touching convex solids: the decision surface is the median plane. That is the right answer at a contact, and no intensity threshold can produce it.
3. It is honest at contacts: `s₁ = s₂ ⇒ p = 0.5`. A method that reports 0.99 on the contact plane is lying.
4. The background class is **essential**. Without `E_∅` the softmax normalizes over granules only, so a voxel 50 µm from everything gets `p=0.34, 0.33, 0.33` — spuriously confident that it belongs to *some* granule.
5. The intensity term is where the **two-color split pays off**: an F-colored voxel gets a huge energy penalty for any I-labelled candidate, so **all F–I contacts are separated by color alone, for free.** With a 50/50 mix and ~6 contacts per granule, roughly half of all contacts are cross-color and trivially solved. Only F–F and I–I contacts need the geometry. Say this out loud in the docs — it halves the difficulty of the central problem.

### 4.2 The `T` correction the user needs to hear

Because the energy is written with a common `1/T`, **annealing `T` alone does not change the MAP labeling at all** — the argmin of `E_k` is `T`-independent. `T` controls only how *soft* the reported probability is. The "strictness" that actually changes the configuration is the shape-slack `τ` and the surface-energy weight `γ̃` (§5). So "relax the strictness" must be implemented as **two schedules**: `T` for the reported/visualized field and the EM soft weights, and `(γ̃, τ)` for the MAP. This is a genuine correction to the specified method, not a nitpick — annealing only `T` would produce a pipeline that appears to relax but whose segmentation never moves.

### 4.3 Centroid-spacing prior (Gibbs point process) — a real energy

The bed is jammed and monodisperse, so the centroid configuration is not a Poisson process. Use a **hard-core + soft-shoulder pair potential with a well at tangency**, on `r = ‖t_k − t_l‖`:

```
r_hard = 2R̄(1 − o_max)     o_max = 0.15   (max tolerated apparent overlap)
r₀     = 2R̄                                (tangency)
r₁     = 2.6 R̄                             (interaction cutoff)

φ(r) = +∞                                       r < r_hard
     = w_rep · ((r₀ − r)/(r₀ − r_hard))²        r_hard ≤ r < r₀      (repulsion)
     = w_att · ((r − r₀)/(r₁ − r₀))²            r₀ ≤ r < r₁          (weak cohesion — the bed is packed)
     = w_att                                     r ≥ r₁

U({t}) = Σ_{k<l} φ(‖t_k − t_l‖),     ∇_{t_k} U = Σ_{l≠k} φ'(r_kl) (t_k − t_l)/r_kl
```

`w_rep ≈ 5 µm`, `w_att ≈ 0.5 µm` (units chosen so `U` adds to the µm-scale energy). Pairs found by `cKDTree.query_pairs(r₁)` — the exact primitive `granule_tessellate.py` already uses.

Two ways to apply it, both recommended:
- **As residual rows** in the joint fit: `ρ_prior[kl] = √(φ(r_kl))` appended per interacting pair (Gauss–Newton on a sum of squares wants roots).
- **As a projection step** between EM iterations: `t_k ← t_k − η ∇_{t_k}U` with backtracking, `η` capped so no centroid moves more than 0.25 voxel per step. The `+∞` branch is implemented as a hard projection: if `r < r_hard`, either push apart to exactly `r_hard` or (preferred) **propose a merge** and let model selection decide.

**The prior does double duty as model selection.** A CellSAM split gives two centroids at `r < r_hard` → forbidden → merge proposal. A CellSAM merge gives one centroid where the local packing wants two → the fit residual for the merged blob is huge and its volume ≈ 2V̄ → split proposal. So the "adaptive fitting scheme with a clustering algorithm that relaxes into the most probable configuration" is precisely: **split/merge moves accepted iff the total Gibbs energy (data + prior + surface) decreases.** That is a well-posed, monotone, terminating procedure — write it exactly that way (§7).

### 4.4 Storage — the memory arithmetic

Concrete realistic volume: `Z=64, Y=X=1024` → **67.1 M voxels** per `(m,t,c)`. Granule 48 px lateral × 16 planes axial → `V̄ ≈ (π/6)(48·48·16) ≈ 1.93×10⁴` voxels; at `φ=0.64`, **K ≈ 2200**.

| representation | arithmetic | size |
|---|---|---|
| dense `K×Z·Y·X` f32 | 2200 × 67.1e6 × 4 B | **590 GB — impossible** |
| per-granule local f32 patch in its own padded bbox | 2200 × 86e3 × 4 B | 760 MB |
| **top-M sparse, M=3**: `ids (Z,Y,X,3) uint16` + `p (Z,Y,X,3) uint8` | 67.1e6 × 3 × 3 B | **604 MB** |
| **top-M sparse, M=2** | 67.1e6 × 2 × 3 B | 403 MB |
| **top-1 + margin**: `ids uint16` + `margin uint8` | 67.1e6 × 3 B | **201 MB** |

**Recommendations, in order:**

1. **Compute:** never materialize globally. Evaluate per z-plane over a **bounded candidate set**: `cand(v)` = granules whose padded bbox contains `v`, obtained from a `cKDTree` on centroids at radius `2R̄ + 2σ_PSF`. In a jammed monodisperse bed the contact number is 6–12, so `|cand| ≤ ~18`; cap `M_cand = 24` and log overflow. Working set = one plane × 24 f32 = 1024·1024·24·4 = **100 MB**, matching the existing per-z-plane discipline in `build_granule_masks`.
2. **Default export (visualization):** top-1 `uint16` label + `uint8` margin `m(v) = round(255(p₁ − p₂))`, **201 MB**. This is exactly what a viewer needs — a label map plus a confidence heat map that lights up as a bright shell on every contact plane. It loses *which* competitor, which does not matter for looking at it.
3. **Export for refinement:** top-M with M=3, **604 MB**. M=3 because at a triple point three granules genuinely meet; ≥4-way coincidences are measure-zero and their mass folds into `p_other = 1 − Σₘ p`, stored implicitly. M=3 is also what makes the ICM refinement affordable (§5.3) — the sparse representation is a compute decision, not just a storage decision.
4. `uint8` quantization of `p` costs 1/510 absolute error, ~50× below the calibration error; f16 is available as an option but is not worth 2× the memory.
5. K > 65535 → raise (uint16 exhausted); document int32 fallback at 2× memory.

```python
@dataclass GranuleProbField:
    ids: np.ndarray       # (Z,H,W,M) uint16, 0 = background/other, granule ids 1..K
    p:   np.ndarray       # (Z,H,W,M) uint8  quantized, descending in M
    m_stored: int
    voxel_size_um: tuple
    temperature_um: float
    def top1(self) -> np.ndarray            # (Z,H,W) uint16
    def margin(self) -> np.ndarray          # (Z,H,W) uint8
    def prob_of(self, gid) -> np.ndarray    # (Z,H,W) float32, densified for ONE granule

def build_prob_field(fits, shape_zhw, voxel_size_um, *, intensity=None, class_of=None,
                     params=None, progress_cb=None) -> GranuleProbField

```

Deps: numpy, `scipy.spatial.cKDTree`. No sklearn/torch. Degraded path: none needed — this is pure numpy.

## 5. `nodegraph/kernels/granule_grow.py` — competitive growth + surface energy

### 5.1 Method choice

**Recommend: multi-source competitive front on a float landscape (compiled watershed as the workhorse), followed by margin-gated MRF-ICM with a Potts term and the shape-slack hinge.** This maps 1:1 onto the user's steps (b) grow and (c) relax.

Rejected alternatives, with reasons:
- **Multi-phase level set / Chan–Vese:** needs `K` coupled level sets → the 590 GB bomb, plus re-initialization and the Vese–Chan phase-coupling trick to avoid vacuum/overlap. And it would spend its effort re-discovering a shape model we already have parametrically. Reject.
- **Pure `skimage.segmentation.watershed`:** it floods by *landscape value*, not accumulated *path cost*, so it can leak through a saddle and its connectivity ignores anisotropic voxel size. But it is compiled C and handles 67 M voxels in ~30 s. **Keep it as the workhorse**, feeding it a landscape that already encodes the metric (below), and reserve the exact path-cost version for validation.
- **Python priority-queue Dijkstra:** correct and metric, but `heapq` at ~10⁶ pushes/s means 40 M contested voxels ≈ 400 s. **Ship it as a reference implementation** used on small contested subvolumes and in tests, not as the main path. If it must scale, use Dial's bucketed queue (integer priority buckets at 0.01 µm quantization → O(1) push/pop).
- **Pure ICM:** exactly the right objective (it is the MAP of §4's model) and cheap per sweep, but local — it needs the front to initialize it.

### 5.2 The front

Seeds: eroded fitted interiors, `S_k = { v : s_k(v) < −ρ_seed }`, `ρ_seed = 0.35 R̄`. These are the "probability-weighted centroids" made concrete: they come from the fits, which came from the probability-weighted M-step. Landscape for the compiled watershed:

```
Λ(v) = min_{k ∈ cand(v)} s_k(v)              # µm; the "nearest surface" scalar field
watershed(Λ, markers=seed_labels, mask=foreground, connectivity=1, compactness=0)
```
Flooding `Λ` from the seeds puts the divide exactly where two `s_k` cross — the median plane. Anisotropy is already inside `s_k` (µm coordinates), which is why feeding a *distance-in-µm* landscape to a connectivity-based flooder is adequate here even though the flooder itself is metric-blind.

For the exact path-cost version (reference/validation), the multi-source Dijkstra edge weight is

```
c_k(u→v) = ‖x_v − x_u‖ · g_k(v),     g_k(v) = exp( s_k(v)/T ) · (1 + λ_I·|I(v) − µ_{c(k)}|/σ_I)
                                                  · (1 + λ_TD·|∇I_TD(v)|/median|∇I_TD|)
D_k(v) = min over paths;   L(v) = argmin_k D_k(v);   p_k(v) = softmax_k(−D_k(v)/T)
```

Leak suppression is quantitative: escaping through a contact neck requires traversing voxels where `s_k = s_neck > 0`, at cost multiplier `exp(s_neck/T)`, so leakage probability decays as `exp(−s_neck·ℓ_neck/T)`. `T` is literally the leak-tightness dial.

**The `λ_TD` term is a recommended addition the user did not ask for.** The file has a `TD` (transmitted-light) channel. Brightfield/DIC shows granule *outlines* — including at contacts, where fluorescence has no signal at all because both solids are uniformly bright. A gradient-magnitude term from TD is the only *direct* observable of a contact plane in this dataset. Test it on synth (if `synth.py` does not model TD, at minimum leave the term with `λ_TD=0` and the hook in place).

### 5.3 ICM update rule

Iterate over the **margin-gated contested set** `Ω = { v : p₁(v) − p₂(v) < 0.5 }` (typically 3–7% of voxels; the full surface shell of thickness `w` is `3w/R̄` ≈ 37% of a granule, and gating on margin cuts that by ~6×), in a flat-index gather/scatter formulation over the top-M candidates:

```
L(v) ← argmin_{k ∈ cand(v)}  [  s_k(v)                                   (data)
                              + γ̃ · Σ_{u∈N₂₆(v)} w_u · 1[L(u) ≠ k]        (Potts / area)
                              + μ · max(0, s_k(v) − τ)²                   (shape slack)
                              + λ_I ℓ ·½(I(v)−µ_{c(k)})ᵀΣ⁻¹(·)            (color) ]
w_u = 1/‖x_u − x_v‖         (metric-correct on anisotropic voxels; Cauchy–Crofton weights
                             if you want the perimeter estimate unbiased rather than just
                             anisotropy-aware)
```

- Monotone: each single-site update strictly decreases the global energy at fixed `(γ̃, τ, T)` ⇒ convergence in 5–10 sweeps. Assert it in a test.
- **Topology guard:** reject any change that would disconnect the granule — the 26-connectivity "simple point" test on the 3×3×3 neighbourhood of `L=k`. Without it, growth sheds islands and the ICM happily creates them. (The existing 26-conn small-component removal in `build_granule_masks` is the post-hoc version; the guard is the in-loop version.)
- Cost: `|Ω| ≈ 3.4 M`, × 26 neighbours × M=3 candidates × 5 sweeps ≈ 1.3×10⁹ element-ops in vectorized numpy ≈ 10 s. Checkerboard (2-color) sweeps to make it safely vectorized.

```python
def grow_competitive(fits, seeds_int32, foreground_zhw, voxel_size_um, *, intensity=None,
                     td=None, params=None, method="watershed", progress_cb=None)
        -> (labels_int32 (Z,H,W), prob GranuleProbField, info dict)
def icm_refine(labels_int32, prob, fits, voxel_size_um, *, gamma_um, tau_um, mu,
               n_sweeps=5, intensity=None, params=None) -> (labels_int32, info)
def dijkstra_reference(...)  # exact path-cost; small volumes / tests only
```

Deps: scipy.ndimage, skimage.segmentation.watershed. Degraded path if skimage absent: use the Dijkstra reference on a `2R̄`-padded per-granule subvolume and stitch by lowest cost — slower but correct.

## 6. Surface energy — turning "no juts" into an energy, with a calibrated weight

Four candidates; the recommendation is a specific combination.

**(A) Boundary-area / Potts — in-loop, primary.** `E_area = γ̃ Σ_{(u,v)} w_uv 1[L(u)≠L(v)]`, discretized as in §5.3. **Why it provably suppresses juts:** a cylindrical protrusion of radius `w` and length `ℓ` gains `ΔV = πw²ℓ` and costs `ΔA = 2πwℓ`, so the energy cost *per voxel of volume gained* is

```
γ̃ ΔA/ΔV = 2γ̃/w   →  ∞  as w → 0
```

Meanwhile the maximum possible data-term gain per voxel is bounded by `|s|_max` (the deepest the voxel can be inside a competitor's surface, ~`R̄` in the worst case but ~`3σ_PSF` in practice near a boundary). So the jut is rejected whenever `2γ̃/w > |s|_gain`, i.e.

```
any protrusion thinner than  w_min = 2γ̃ / |s|_gain  is strictly rejected.
⇒  set  γ̃ = w_min · |s|_gain / 2       with w_min = 0.15 R̄, |s|_gain = 3σ_PSF
```

That is a **derivation of the penalty weight from a physical statement**, not a tuned constant. Ship it as the default and log the implied `w_min` so the user can see what they are forbidding.

**(C) Shape-slack hinge to the fitted superellipsoid — in-loop, primary.** `E_shape = μ Σ_{v: L(v)=k} max(0, s_k(v) − τ)²`. This is *exactly* the user's "relax the strictness to allow variation in granule features": voxels are allowed to sit outside the fitted surface up to a slack `τ`, and beyond that they pay quadratically. And `τ` is not a free knob — it is **measured from the data** as `τ = 3σ_rough` from §1.7. Recommend this as the primary shape term because `s_k` is already computed, it is parametric (so it forbids juts of *any* width, not just thin ones), and its slack has a physical meaning.

**(D) EDT medial thickness — out-of-loop cleanup.** Per granule, `thick(v) = 2·distance_transform_edt(mask_k, sampling=d)`. Prune any connected sub-part whose maximum thickness `< w_min`. Cheap (one EDT per bbox, the exact call `granule_volume_mask.py` already makes) and it catches residual juts that survived the local ICM. Recommend as a final pass alongside the existing small-component removal.

**(B) Mean curvature / Willmore `γ_H ∫H²dA` — diagnostic only.** Suppresses high curvature more sharply than area and is scale-aware, but the voxel discretization of `H = ∇·(∇φ/|∇φ|)` is noisy at 1-voxel scale, and doing it properly needs a mesh. **Compute it once at the end on the marching-cubes mesh** with the cotangent Laplacian (`H n ≈ (1/2A_v)Σ(cot α + cot β)(v − v_j)`) and report `∫H²dA / (surface area)` as a per-granule shape-quality diagnostic. Do not put it in the loop.

### 6.1 Annealing schedule ("relax the strictness")

Two independent schedules, per §4.2:

```
i = 0..n−1,  n = 6
T_i  = T₀ q^i,      T₀ = 3(σ_PSF + σ_rough),  T_end ≥ 0.5·max(d),  q = 0.7      # reported softness
τ_i  = τ₀ + (τ_max − τ₀)·i/(n−1),   τ₀ = 0.5σ_rough,  τ_max = 3σ_rough           # shape slack: strict → relaxed
γ̃_i  = max( γ̃₀ q^i , γ̃_min ),  γ̃_min = w_min·3σ_PSF/2                            # smoothness: smooth → detailed, floored
```

Start strict and smooth (the segmentation is essentially the fitted superellipsoids), then progressively admit roughness. `γ̃` is floored at the calibrated value so that **no schedule stage can ever admit a jut thinner than `w_min`** — the guarantee survives annealing. Log the total energy at fixed `(γ̃,τ)` and assert monotone decrease within a stage; across stages the objective changes so energy may rise — log, do not assert.

## 7. `nodegraph/kernels/granule_relax.py` — the EM driver (this is where the circularity dies)

```python
@dataclass GranuleSolution:
    fits: dict[int, SuperellipsoidFit]
    labels: np.ndarray            # (Z,H,W) int32
    prob: GranuleProbField
    class_of: dict[int, int]      # 0=I, 1=F
    energy_trace: list[dict]
    info: dict

def relax_granules(foreground_zhw, seeds_int32, intensity_zyxc, voxel_size_um,
                   *, r_known_um, params=None, progress_cb=None) -> GranuleSolution
```

```
init:  fits_k = moment_init(seed voxels, w=1, R̄)              # closed form, no optimizer
for i in 0..n−1:                                              # schedule stage i
  E-step:   prob = build_prob_field(fits, T=T_i, class_of, intensity)
  M-step-1: for each k:  fits_k = fit_superellipsoid(
                             points in padded bbox_k,
                             weights = p_k(v),                 # SOFT, not a hard mask
                             init = fits_k, r_known_um = R̄)
            ← this is the user's "centroid from the probability, weights controlled by
              the known geometry": the weights are p_k, and the known geometry enters
              through ρ_size and ρ_aspect in the residual.
  M-step-2: centroids ← project_centroids(centroids, R̄)        # Gibbs prior, §4.3
  labels:   grow_competitive(fits, seeds from eroded fits) ; icm_refine(γ̃_i, τ_i)
  moves:    propose splits (rms_k > 3·median rms  OR  V_k > 1.6V̄)
                  → 2-means on the voxels with s_k > τ, refit both
            propose merges (r_kl < r_hard  OR  the pair's joint fit beats the two singles)
            accept iff total energy  Σ_v E_{L(v)}(v) + γ̃|∂L| + μΣhinge + U({t})  decreases
  stop if max |Δt_k| < 0.25·max(d) and label-change fraction < 1e-3
```

**Why this resolves the circularity.** The user's sequence is "assign centroids from the probability" + "probability computed from the geometry (centroids)". As stated it is a fixed-point equation with no initializer and no guarantee of a fixed point. Written as **block-coordinate descent (EM) on one explicit energy**, it becomes well-posed: a defined initializer (EDT seeds → closed-form moment fits, *no probability required*), an E-step and two M-steps that each provably reduce the same scalar, and a termination criterion. The split/merge moves are the "clustering algorithm that relaxes the estimate into the most probable configuration" — a split-merge EM / reversible-jump-lite. Nothing is lost from the user's intent; what is added is the initializer, the objective, and the proof of termination.

Complexity per stage: E-step 100 MB working set, ~20 s; M-step-1 11 s; growth 30 s; ICM 10 s. Six stages ≈ **7 min per (m,t)**. That is the budget to design against.

## 8. `nodegraph/kernels/granule_dots.py` — the rotation observable

### 8.1 Detection

Reuse `kernels/bead_detect.detect_beads(volume_zhw, voxel_size_um, params)` on the GFP channel with `method="log"`, `bead_radius = r_dot/dx`, `min_size/max_size` bracketing the dot volume — it already implements 3D LoG (`_fspecial_log`), `_subpixel_poly_3d` parabolic refinement, radial-symmetry refinement, and NMS (`_suppress_close`). Do not write a new detector.

Set the LoG scale anisotropically: `σ_axis = r_dot/(√3 · d_axis)`. Subvoxel accuracy is not cosmetic — it *is* the rotation error budget:

```
per-dot angular error ≈ σ_d / R̄
n-dot Kabsch error    σ_θ ≈ σ_d / (R̄ √n)          (roughly; exact form in §8.4)
example: R̄ = 24 px, σ_d = 0.3 px, n = 8  →  σ_θ ≈ 0.0044 rad = 0.25°
```

### 8.2 Parent binding

Signed distance to a fitted surface is essentially free given §1:

```
u = R_kᵀ(x_dot − t_k);   s_k = ‖u‖(1 − F(u)^(−ε₁/2))
```

A dot stuck on the outside has its centroid at radial offset `r_off ≈ r_dot` (0 if half-embedded) — estimate `r_off` globally as the median `s` over unambiguous dots, as a nuisance parameter.

```
k*(x) = argmin_{k : class_k = F, ‖x − t_k‖ < 1.5R̄}  | s_k(x) − r_off |
σ_s   = √(σ_rough² + σ_d² + σ_fit²)                    (σ_fit from cov_pose)
reject   if  min |s − r_off| > 3σ_s                    → flag ORPHAN
ambiguous if second-best is within 2σ_s of the best    → flag AMBIGUOUS, keep (re-resolve
                                                          after poses converge, don't drop)
contradiction if k* is class I                         → flag CROSSTALK, count it
```
The orphan rate and the crosstalk rate are *validation statistics on real data* (§10.3). Skip Euclidean footpoint refinement (Newton along `∇F`): the radial-vs-Euclidean discrepancy is second-order in surface tilt (`≤ (1−cos θ)ρ`), and for an *argmin over parents* only the ordering matters.

### 8.3 Body-frame coordinates

Store both:
```
p_j = R_kᵀ(x_j − t_k)                         # (3,) µm Cartesian body frame → used by Kabsch
(η_j, ω_j) = body_surface_coords(x_j, fit_k)   # (2,) intrinsic → used for identity & display
```
`(η,ω)` is the correct *identity* coordinate across frames because it is invariant to the small frame-to-frame drift in fitted `a,b,c,ε` that would otherwise make a stationary dot appear to move in Cartesian body coords.

### 8.4 Rotation by orthogonal Procrustes (Kabsch)

Given matched template body positions `P = [p₁..p_n]ᵀ (n×3)` and observed centered world positions `q_j = x_j − t_k(t)`:

```
minimize Σ_j w_j ‖R p_j − q_j‖²   over R ∈ SO(3)
H = Pᵀ W Q            (3×3),  W = diag(w)
U, Σ, Vᵀ = svd(H)
D = diag(1, 1, sign(det(V Uᵀ)))         ← reflection guard: WITHOUT this, noisy near-planar
R = V D Uᵀ                                 constellations return a reflection (det = −1),
                                           which is not a rotation and will corrupt the track
rms = √( Σ w_j‖Rp_j − q_j‖² / Σ w_j )
```

**Uncertainty, exactly.** Linearize `q ≈ (I + [δ]×)p`; the residual Jacobian w.r.t. `δ` is `−[p_j]×`, so

```
cov(δ) = σ_d² · ( Σ_j w_j ( ‖p_j‖² I − p_j p_jᵀ ) )⁻¹
```

The bracketed matrix is the **inertia tensor of the dot constellation**. Its eigendecomposition tells you which rotation axis is poorly determined and by how much — a rigorous, per-granule, per-frame observability test. (Note: `aldvc_field.py:786` is the repo's only existing `eigh`; `so3.py` and this will be the others.) Minimum dot counts fall straight out of the rank:

| n dots | rank of the inertia matrix | status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | rotation about that dot's direction is free — **1 DOF unresolved** |
| 2, non-parallel & non-antipodal | 3 | **determined**, unchecked |
| 2, collinear with center | 2 | still 1 DOF unresolved |
| 3, not all on one great circle through the center | 3 | determined + 1 redundancy → outlier detectable |
| ≥ 4 | 3 | RANSAC possible |

**Below 2 usable dots:** do *not* fabricate a number. Fall back to the body-shape orientation with the `G`-branch resolved by temporal continuity, and if `symmetry_group.kind == "SO2_z"` report the free azimuth as `NaN` with `flags |= AZIMUTH_UNOBSERVABLE`. A pipeline that silently emits a confident angle here is the worst possible failure, because it looks like data.

### 8.5 Correspondence across frames

Nearest-neighbour in world space fails as soon as per-frame rotation exceeds the dot spacing. Recommend:

1. Coarse `R̂` from the body fit (all `G` candidates) or from `R̂_{t−1}·exp(ω̂Δt)` (constant-ω extrapolation).
2. **`scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment`** on `C_{jm} = ‖R̂ p_j − q_m‖²`, padded with dummy rows/columns at gate cost `c_gate = (3σ_d + ‖R̂ error‖R̄)²` so dots may disappear (bleaching) or appear. Hungarian on `n ≤ 30` is free.
3. Kabsch on the matches → new `R̂` → re-match. 2–3 iterations (ICP with known point sets; converges fast).
4. Repeat 2–3 for **every `g ∈ G`** and keep the lowest `rms` → this *is* the 4-group resolution.
5. **RANSAC** when `rms > 3σ_d`: sample 3 matches, Kabsch, count inliers within `3σ_d`, keep best, refit on inliers. Sample count for 99% success at 60% inlier rate with 3-point models: `log(0.01)/log(1 − 0.6³) = 19` → **20 iterations**. Negligible cost.

**Template maintenance:** keep a per-granule body template, updated after each frame's `R` is fixed:
```
p_j ← (1−α)p_j + α R_tᵀ q_j,  α = 0.2,  then re-project onto the surface: p_j ← p_j · (r_off + ρ(p̂_j))/‖p_j‖
```
The re-projection is a constrained mean that prevents the template from drifting off the surface. With a **fixed** template, `R_template→world(t)` is *absolute* orientation, so for F granules the 4-group ambiguity is gone entirely and there is no accumulated drift.

**This yields a real-data validation with no ground truth:** on F granules, compare the dot-derived pose to the body-shape-fit pose (reduced by `G`). They are independent estimators of the same quantity. Their disagreement bounds the body-only estimator's error on real data. That check costs nothing and is worth more than any synthetic number.

```python
@dataclass Dot: id, parent_id, pos_um (3,), body_um (3,), eta, omega, s_um, intensity, flags
def detect_dots(gfp_zhw, voxel_size_um, *, r_dot_um, params) -> (points_um (Nd,3), rows)

        -> (pairs (n,2) int, R (3,3), rms_um, cov_delta (3,3), inliers bool[n])
def pose_from_dots(dots_by_frame, fits_by_frame, group, params)
        -> (quats (T,4), cov (T,3,3), n_used (T,), flags (T,))
```
Deps: numpy, scipy (`linear_sum_assignment`, `svd`), `kernels/bead_detect`. No sklearn/torch. Degraded path: no dots detected → the whole module returns empty and the pipeline falls back to body-only pose with `G`-quotient output; that must be a normal code path, not an error.

## 9. `nodegraph/kernels/granule_classify.py` — F vs I

### 9.1 Minimum general primitive

```python
def object_channel_stats(labels_zhw, channels_zyxc, *, erode_voxels=2, fits=None,
                         stats=("mean","median","p10","p90","std","n")) -> np.ndarray  # (K,C,S)
def classify_two_population(stats, *, feature="log_ratio", ch_a=0, ch_b=1,
                            depth_correct=True, centroids_um=None, params=None)
        -> (cls (K,) int8, prob (K,2) f32, info dict)
```

**Erode before measuring.** PSF bleed across a contact plane is the dominant error in per-object intensity: two touching granules of different colors each acquire ~2σ_PSF of the other's signal at the interface, and a naive mean pulls both toward the middle. Measure on the eroded core (`erode_voxels=2`) or, better, on `{v : s_k(v) < −2σ_PSF}` using the fit. This one line is the difference between 99.5% and 90% accuracy.

### 9.2 Decision rule

Feature: `z_k = log( (median_A(k) + δ) / (median_B(k) + δ) )`, `δ` = the background estimate (1 DN floor).

Rule: **2-component 1-D `sklearn.mixture.GaussianMixture` on `{z_k}`, with a BIC guard over `n_components ∈ {1,2,3}`**, posteriors → `prob`.

Why log-ratio: constant fluorescence means per-object intensity = (true brightness) × (depth attenuation) × (bleaching) × (excitation nonuniformity). The middle factors are largely **channel-common multiplicative nuisances**, and a ratio cancels them; the log makes what remains two shifted Gaussians of comparable variance. In linear ratio space the same mixture is skewed and heteroscedastic, so any symmetric test is biased.

Why GMM over Otsu: (i) Otsu gives a hard threshold with **no confidence** — but downstream you need `P(F)` to reason about "an F granule with zero detected dots" vs "an I granule with 3 dots", which are diagnostically different; (ii) Otsu assumes roughly balanced classes and equal variances; a GMM handles unequal populations and variances; (iii) **the BIC over `{1,2,3}` components is a real safeguard**: if BIC selects 1, the two channels are *not* separating the populations and you must raise rather than silently bisecting noise into two "populations". That guard alone justifies the choice.

Depth correction: attenuation makes `z_k` drift with centroid depth. Regress `z_k = α + β·z_centroid` (Huber or plain OLS with 3σ trimming), subtract the trend, then fit the GMM. Recommended default `True` for real data, `False` for synth unless synth models attenuation.

Circularity guard: classify on `z` **first**, bind dots **second**, then at most one optional consistency pass (2-D GMM on `(z_k, n_dots_k)`). Do not make the primary decision depend on dots, since dot binding needs the class.

Deps: sklearn (`GaussianMixture`). **Degraded path:** `histogram_threshold.compute_histogram` + `suggest_threshold_otsu` on `z` (already in the repo), returning hard labels with `prob ∈ {0,1}` and `info["degraded"]=True`.

## 11. Honest assessment

### Mathematically sound as stated
- **Geometric, not intensity-based, probability at contacts.** Correct and necessary. Nearest-signed-distance assignment is the provably right estimator for two identical touching convex solids; the decision surface is the median plane. Intensity cannot do this, ever, for same-color contacts.
- **Known granule size as a prior.** Strong. Best used as `ρ_size` on the absolute scale, with eigenvalue *ratios* carrying the shape — because ratios survive contact truncation and absolute eigenvalues do not.
- **Surface energy penalizing high-aspect-ratio juts.** Sound, and the `ΔA/ΔV = 2γ̃/w` argument makes it quantitative enough to *derive* the penalty weight from a stated minimum feature width.
- **Centroid-spacing prior.** Sound; it is a Gibbs hard-core/soft-shoulder point process, and it does double duty as split/merge model selection.
- **Dots as the rotation observable.** Correct and essential. Without them, F and I granules alike would be limited to orientation-mod-`G` with a 45°/frame Nyquist ceiling.
- **Per-voxel visualizable field.** Sound, and the top-1+margin export makes it cheap.

### Needs reformulating
1. **The circularity is real.** "Assign centroids from the probability" + "probability from the centroids" is a fixed-point equation with no initializer and no proof of a fixed point. **Fix: EM / block-coordinate descent on one explicit energy** (§7), initialized from EDT h-maxima + closed-form moment fits, which require no probability. The user's step (a) then becomes literally the M-step with `w_v = p_k(v)`.
2. **"CellSAM produces the initial segmentation" is the weakest link.** It is 2D-only, returns labels with **no probability** (`mask_threshold` eats the logits inside the kernel), and is trained on membrane-bounded cells — not filled, constant-intensity, densely contacting solids. It will merge contacting granules, which is exactly the case that matters. **Reformulate: CellSAM → foreground only; EDT h-maxima at the known radius → instance count and seeds.** For a jammed bed of known-size convex solids the EDT interior maxima *are* the centroids and the contacts *are* the saddles; it is five lines and it will very likely beat CellSAM here. Run both, reconcile (EDT splits CellSAM components), and gate on the geometric count `φV_fg/V̄`. If soft CellSAM output is genuinely wanted later, it requires modifying `cellsam_segment.py` to surface pre-threshold logits — a separate, non-trivial change.
3. **"Relax the strictness" via `T` does not do what it sounds like.** With the energy written with a single `1/T`, annealing `T` leaves the MAP labeling *exactly unchanged*; it only softens the reported probability. The strictness that moves the segmentation is `(γ̃, τ)`. Two schedules, not one (§6.1). Without this correction you would build something that appears to relax and never actually changes its answer.
4. **"Grow the boundary" must be competitive, not per-granule.** Independent growth of `K` granules in a contacting bed produces overlapping volumes and a "probability" that does not normalize. Multi-source competition on a shared landscape is required; growth must be able to *lose* voxels, not only gain them.
5. **Softmax-over-SDF is a Gibbs posterior, not a calibrated frequentist probability.** If "probability" is to be taken literally by a viewer, `T` must be calibrated: fit `T` by maximizing voxel-wise log-likelihood against synth ground truth and report ECE / Brier / a reliability diagram. Until then, label the field "confidence" in the UI, not "probability".
6. **Roughness and exponents are partially confounded.** A rough sphere is well fit by (`ε` slightly off + small roughness) or (correct `ε` + larger roughness). Mitigations: robust `soft_l1` loss so roughness cannot drive `ε`; the `ρ_eps` shrinkage prior; and reporting `ε` **with its covariance**, falling back to `ε=1` when the covariance is large. Empirically expect `ε` to be unidentifiable once `σ_rough > 0.15R̄` — validate, do not gate.
7. **Anisotropic Z is the binding constraint.** With `dz/dxy` of 3–5 (typical confocal), rotation about in-plane axes will be systematically worse than about `z` by roughly that ratio. Report `σ_θ` per axis (you get it free from `cov(δ)`), and expect the anisotropy — do not treat it as a bug.

### Where this will fail on real data
- **CellSAM merges** at F–F and I–I contacts (mitigated by EDT seeds).
- **Large contact areas.** If granules are soft/deformed, the contact is a *facet*, not a point, and the median-plane assumption is biased — both granules are truncated and both fits shrink. The contact-voxel down-weighting (§1.7) mitigates; nothing fully fixes it without a deformable model.
- **Depth attenuation** drifts the F/I log-ratio with `z` (mitigated by the depth regression) and degrades deep dot detection, so rotation quality will be depth-dependent. Report per-depth `σ_θ`.
- **Dot sparsity.** F granules with <2 visible dots have undetermined rotation. Report the coverage fraction; expect it to fall with depth and with bleaching over the time series.
- **Non-superellipsoidal granules** (chipped, aggregated, doublets). Must have a reject/flag path (`rms > 3×median` → `SHAPE_OUTLIER`), never a forced fit.
- **18.5 GB / 4 channels.** You cannot hold multiple float copies of anything. The top-M sparse representation and the per-z-plane evaluation are not optimizations, they are requirements.

### Minimum viable core

Everything below uses only numpy/scipy/skimage — **no torch, no CellSAM, no optimizer**, and it produces all three deliverables correctly:

# PART 3 — The tessellation investigation


## A. Theory + numerics

> Never independently refuted; numerically self-verified. Overturned SS D's false 'a kD-tree cannot answer a power query' claim.

## VERDICT (one line)

Yes — nearest-surface/SDF-argmax **is** a tessellation (the Set Voronoi / navigational tessellation, in its exact sampling-free form). And for the labelling of *granule* voxels it is provably **pixel-identical** to the 2D power/Laguerre diagram, which we can compute exactly **169× faster** with a two-line change to a kernel we already ship. Ordinary Voronoi on centroids is disqualified by a factor of ~10 in error.

## 1. The central claim: refuted as stated, then rescued by a stronger theorem

**The two surfaces are genuinely different.** With `s_i(x) = |x−c_i| − R_i`:

- `s_1 = s_2` ⟺ `|x−c1| − |x−c2| = R1 − R2` → a **hyperboloid of revolution** (two sheets, foci `c1,c2`), with `a = |R1−R2|/2`, `c = d/2`, `b² = c² − a² = (d² − (R1−R2)²)/4`. At tangency (`d = R1+R2`) this collapses to the clean form **`b² = R1·R2`**, sheet: `x1 = d/2 + a·√(1 + ρ²/(R1R2))`.
- Power/radical: `|x−c1|² − R1² = |x−c2|² − R2²` → the **plane** `x1 = (d² + R1² − R2²)/(2d)`. Genuinely affine (an "affine diagram", Aurenhammer 1987).

**(a) Equal radii.** `a = 0` ⇒ the hyperboloid degenerates to the plane `x1 = d/2`; the radical plane is also `x1 = d/2`; ordinary Voronoi likewise. All three coincide **exactly**, and at tangency `d/2 = R1` = the contact point, normal to the centre line ⇒ it *is* the common tangent plane. This is exactly why the literature says classical Voronoi "only yields precise results for monodisperse spheres" (Weis/Schönhöfer/Schaller/Schröter/Schröder-Turk).

**(b) Unequal radii — the number that decides it.**

First, a fact I verified numerically to 1e-13 and which the brief's framing misses: **both bisectors always contain `∂B1 ∩ ∂B2`**, because there `s_1 = s_2 = 0` *and* `pow_1 = pow_2 = 0`. At tangency that locus is the contact point; the radical plane sits at `x1 = R1` exactly and the hyperboloid *vertex* is at `d/2 + a = R1` — the same point. They are anchored together and only diverge away from the contact.

Off-axis separation at tangency:

```
Δ(ρ) = ((R1−R2)/2)·(√(1 + ρ²/(R1R2)) − 1)   ≈ (R1−R2)ρ²/(4R1R2)   for small ρ
Δ(ρ=√(R1R2)) = 0.2071·(R1−R2)
```

With interpenetration `δ = R1+R2−d`, the on-axis separation has an exact closed form (verified):

```
x1_hyp − x1_radical = −(R1−R2)·δ / (2d)
```

| 37.5, 9 | 0.66 vox | 3.4 px (3.9 %) |
| 69.7, 8.5 | 0.82 vox | 4.2 px (5.4 %) |

**Answer: sub-voxel to ~2 voxels, and ≤1 voxel where it matters (inside the solid, at contacts).** For scale, the *ordinary Voronoi* error is `(R1−R2)/2`: p50 = 8.43 µm = **4.91 vox**, p90 = 17.71 µm = **10.31 vox**, max 27.94 µm = **16.26 vox** — the larger disc penetrates its own bisector by up to 3.7× the *smaller* disc's radius. Hierarchy: `|Voronoi error| ≫ |power − nearest-surface|`.

Also decisive: `∂x1_radical/∂R1 = R1/d`, so a **1-voxel error in the fitted radius** moves the boundary by `1.72 × 37.5/46.5 = 1.39 µm = 0.81 voxel` — *as large as the entire power-vs-hyperboloid difference*. Arguing about which bisector is second-order next to fitting R well.

**(c) Which is physically correct: both, and provably so.**

> **Theorem (exactness on the solid).** Let `B_1…B_K` be closed sets with pairwise disjoint interiors. For `x ∈ int B_i \ ⋃_{j≠i} B_j`: `s_i(x) < 0 ≤ s_j(x)` for all `j≠i`, hence `argmin_k s_k(x) = i`. The identical argument with `pow_i` (`pow_i(x) < 0 ≤ pow_j(x)`) holds for balls. ∎
>
> **Corollaries.** (i) Each body lies entirely inside its own cell in *both* diagrams — no poke-out, `φ_local ≤ 1` by construction. (ii) The two diagrams therefore **agree with each other on every voxel inside a body**. (iii) The signed-distance version needs only closedness — no sphericity, no convexity — so it survives superellipsoids and even concave shapes.

Verified on a synthetic bed drawn from the *measured* radius distribution (N=453, R 8.5–67.2 µm, R_max/R_min = 9.7, φ = 0.279, 1024² raster at 1.7183 µm/px):

| diagram | solid px misassigned | per-disc area lost (mean / p90 / max) | discs losing >5 % |
|---|---|---|---|
| ordinary Voronoi | **5.27 %** | 1.67 % / 5.99 % / **26.2 %** | 55 / 453 |
| power / Laguerre | **0.000 %** | 0 / 0 / 0 | 0 |
| signed-distance (Set Voronoi) | **0.000 %** | 0 / 0 / 0 | 0 |

At φ = 0.416: Voronoi 8.12 % misassigned, max 44.6 % of a disc lost, 124 discs >5 %; the largest 20 % of discs lose 10.2 % on average (the error is systematically *against the large granules*).

And the headline: **power vs signed-distance disagree on 9.4 % of all space but 0.000 % inside the solid** — 100 % of the disagreement is in the empty medium (13.0 % of void voxels). After masking to foreground they are the *same labelling*.

## 3. Set Voronoi ≡ our SDF-argmax

**Yes, plainly.** Unsigned nearest-surface assignment is by definition the Voronoi diagram of the sets `∂B_i`. Discretising `∂B_i` with spacing `h` introduces a sagitta error `≈ h²/(8R)` (0.024 µm at h = 1.9 µm, R = 19 µm — negligible); the operative error is bisector discretisation. Measured convergence to the exact signed-distance argmin:

| pts/disc | spacing | agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 8.6 vox | 97.08 % |
| 16 | 4.3 vox | 99.19 % |
| 32 | 2.2 vox | 99.79 % |
| 64 | 1.1 vox | **99.95 %** |

So the planned SDF-argmax is the **exact, sampling-free Set Voronoi tessellation, plus a sign fix**. We therefore inherit: `φ_local ≤ 1` by construction; well-definedness for aspherical shapes; reduction to ordinary Voronoi in the monodisperse limit; and Schaller et al.'s result that the `φ_local` distribution correlates with global packing fraction.

**For superellipsoids** nothing in the SDF route breaks — the theorem used no sphericity. What breaks is only the *power-diagram shortcut*. Fallbacks, in order: (i) power diagram of the **circumscribed spheres** as a cheap superset of the adjacency (the true nearest-surface boundary between two convex bodies can only lie where the circumsphere cells touch — I verified this only for spheres, **flagging as unproven for superquadrics**); (ii) anisotropic power diagram of the fitted covariance; (iii) surface-point Set Voronoi at ~1-voxel spacing.

**One correction to the plan's stated identity.** `argmax_k p_k = argmin_k s_k` holds *only* when the intensity and log-prior terms are equal across `k` — i.e. same colour class and equal `π_k`. In general `argmax p` minimises `s_k + λ_I·(…)·ℓ − ℓ log π_k`. Since `|∇s| = 1` for a true SDF, an energy imbalance of `ΔE` µm displaces the boundary by exactly `ΔE` µm — so **any intensity/prior term exceeding 1.72 µm moves the boundary more than one voxel**. That is your calibration for `λ_I`. Likewise "decision surface at the median plane" is true only for `R1 = R2`; in general it is the hyperboloid. The *estimator* claim survives because both are exact on the solid.

## 4. What a tessellation buys that a per-voxel field does not

1. **A 169× cheaper exact algorithm.** See §6. Same labels, 0.06 s vs 10.71 s at 1024²/K=453.
2. **Exact adjacency for the Gibbs prior.** Lifted hull → 1338 truly-adjacent pairs in 2.4 ms. `cKDTree.query_pairs(r=1.3·2R_max)` → 2789 pairs in 0.3 ms: **2.08× over-generation**, and it requires a cutoff `r1` keyed to `R_max` (wasteful) or a per-pair loop. The power adjacency is exactly the set of pairs that *can* share a boundary — the correct support for a pair potential — and is parameter-free.
3. **Exact analytic cell areas/volumes.** A 9 µm-radius granule is 86 px with a 33 px perimeter; a systematic **half-voxel** bias in surface placement costs ~16 px = **19 % of the smallest granules' area**. Polygonal cells remove that quantisation entirely.
4. **A real per-cell error detector** — but *not* the one you'd guess. Plain `φ_i = |B_i| / |cell_i|` is **merge-invariant** for equal partners (merging two radius-R discs into one radius-√2R fit leaves `φ` unchanged), so it is *not* a merge/split discriminator. What works is the **unexplained-foreground residual**

   ```
   η_i = |{v ∈ cell_i : foreground}| / |cell_i|      (cell fill)
   φ_i = |B_i| / |cell_i|                            (own occupancy)
   r_i = η_i − φ_i  ∈ [−1, 1]
   ```
   `r_i = 0` for a correct segmentation; a **missing seed** drives `r_i` up by exactly the missing object's area / |cell_i|, and — crucially — **localises the error to one cell**. A voxel field gives you this only after you have defined the cells, i.e. you need the tessellation.
5. **Topological consistency invariants.** In 2D any normal tessellation with degree-3 vertices satisfies `E = 3F − 6`; measured 1338 edges vs `3·454 − 6 = 1356` (gap from degree-4 vertices + border). Mean neighbours/cell = 5.91 (hull) / 6.00 (interior raster). A voxel field supplies no V/E/F at all and cannot detect a disconnected or vanished cell.
6. **Coordination number — with a hard caveat.** Because mean adjacency in 2D is pinned to 6 by Euler, **the mean cell degree carries zero packing information in 2D** and must not be used as a validation statistic. The physical contact number is a *gap-gated* subgraph: frictionless isostatic `Z = 2d` → **4 in 2D**, 6 in 3D; frictional fully-mobilised `Z = d+1` → **3 in 2D**, 4 in 3D. What *is* informative is the **distribution** of edges per cell (Richard & Gervois showed the per-face edge-number distribution separates disordered packings).

## 5. Where tessellation is actively wrong or dangerous here

1. **It partitions all of space.** 72 % of our plane is void at φ = 0.28, and every void voxel gets a granule label; 13 % of void voxels even differ between the two rules. Masking is non-negotiable. **The elegant point: the planned `E_empty = m0` background class is exactly the repair** — it is what converts a tessellation back into a segmentation. Keep it; it is not optional decoration.
2. **It cannot decide K.** One generator per object, full stop. A missing seed enlarges a neighbour *silently* and by exactly the missing object's area. Our 446 z=1 components already contain merges. Only `r_i` (above) catches it.
3. **2D-per-plane means z-adjacency is meaningless.** Measured plane IoU 0.06–0.20; nearest z+1 centroid 41.5 µm away, > one median diameter. Stacking per-plane tessellations is **not** a 3D tessellation and the 3D Euler/coordination invariants do not transfer.
4. **Stereology kills the jamming assumption.** For a random planar section of a 3D packing the expected areal fraction equals the volume fraction (Delesse 1847 / Rosiwal; Weibel). Our 0.28 in-plane ⇒ `φ_3D ≈ 0.28` at z=1, falling to 0.014 at z=6 — which is *depth attenuation*, not physics. Either way, 0.28 is far below random loose packing (~0.55) and far below 2D MRJ discs (~0.83). **We cannot establish that this bed is jammed, so no Z-based prior or Z-based validation is defensible on this data.** (Also: it is definitively *not* a 2D monolayer — a jammed polydisperse monolayer would read φ ≳ 0.84.)
5. **Border truncation silently violates the theorems.** In my synthetic raster, clipping cells at the frame produced `φ_i` up to **1.25** for the power diagram and **1.69** for Voronoi — a spurious violation of `φ_i ≤ 1` caused *purely* by truncation. Every `φ_i`/Euler/`r_i` statistic must be restricted to cells whose closure does not touch the frame. `scipy.spatial.Voronoi` returns `-1` vertices for unbounded cells; the repo's kernel skips them → NaN volume, which silently drops all border granules from statistics. `analysis.voronoi`'s existing `bound ∈ [per_region, mask, frame]` lever is the right place to handle this (ghost/mirror generators or a bounding box).
6. **Power-diagram-specific guards.** Assert every cell is non-empty and contains its own centre; if fitted radii are wrong enough to nest, a granule's cell can vanish with no error raised.

## 6. The operative 2D case: exact recipe

**The lift, stated exactly.** For discs `(c_i, R_i)` in `R^d`, `w_i = R_i²`:

```
pow_i(x) = |x|² − 2c_i·x + (|c_i|² − R_i²) = |x|² − h_i(x),   h_i(x) = 2c_i·x − (|c_i|² − R_i²)
argmin_i pow_i = argmax_i h_i
```
So the power diagram is the projection of the **upper envelope of K hyperplanes** in `R^{d+1}`; equivalently its dual **regular (weighted Delaunay)** triangulation is the projection of the **lower convex hull of the lifted points `p_i = (c_i, |c_i|² − R_i²)`**. Complexity `O(n log n)` in 2D (Aurenhammer 1987).

**scipy has no power diagram.** `Voronoi`/`Delaunay` take no weights (verified: 6.2 % edge disagreement). So:

**(i) Combinatorics — hand-write the lift (30 lines).**
```python
lift = np.column_stack([C, (C**2).sum(1) - R**2])
hull = ConvexHull(lift)
low  = [s for s, eq in zip(hull.simplices, hull.equations) if eq[-2] < 0]   # downward facets
```
Measured: **2.4 ms** for K=453 → 886 triangles, 1338 edges, mean degree 5.91. Cell *polygons* additionally need power vertices = orthocentres (one 2×2 solve per triangle from `pow_i = pow_j = pow_k`).

**(ii) Raster labels — do NOT hand-write anything. Use the exact reduction to a plain nearest-neighbour query.** Let `H = max_i R_i`. Lift sites to `(c_i, √(H² − R_i²))` and queries to `(x, 0)`:
```
|(x,0) − (c_i, √(H²−R_i²))|² = |x−c_i|² + H² − R_i² = pow_i(x) + H²
```
The offset `H²` is site-independent ⇒ **argmin is identical**. Verified: **0 mismatches out of 1,048,576** pixels against brute-force power argmin.

| method | 1024², K=453 |
|---|---|
| brute-force power argmin (chunked numpy) | **10.71 s** |
| brute-force signed-distance argmin | ~6.7 s (adds a sqrt) |
| **cKDTree lift (exact power diagram)** | **0.06 s** — 169× |
| ordinary Voronoi via cKDTree | 0.05 s |
| 3D, 7×1024×1024 = 7.34 M voxels, 4D lift | **0.33 s** (K=400) / 0.37 s (K=2000) |

`nodegraph/catalog/analysis/voronoi.py:86` already does `cKDTree(seeds_vox[pick] * sc)` then `tree.query(pts, k=1, …)` in µm space. **Appending one weight column to the seeds and one zero column to the queries turns that node into an exact Laguerre tessellation** — dimension-generic, since the trick is `d → d+1` for any `d`. One caveat to handle: `distance_upper_bound` semantics change — a power-distance cap `p_max` maps to a lifted bound `√(p_max + H²)`, and the returned `dist` is `√(pow + H²)`, not a distance. `max_distance_um` must be converted or its meaning documented.

**Engineering verdict: the kD-tree lift, not the raster field.** It is exact, 169× cheaper, and reuses shipped code. Reserve the ConvexHull lift for what the raster *cannot* give: adjacency, analytic areas, and the Euler check.

## 7. The dead merge loop: keep the scaffold, kill the criterion

**Assessment: leave `merge_tol` pinned at 0.0.** The *machinery* is right-shaped — iterative RAG, union-find (higher id → lower), rebuild-and-restart on boundary failure, fixpoint driver. The *objective* is wrong, for three independent reasons:

1. **`|d_i − d_j| / max(d_i,d_j)` with `d = n_points/enclosed_volume` compares sampling artefacts, not evidence.** Our point densities are set by how densely we sampled the fitted surface, not by anything physical: the same granule sampled at two densities scores as two different objects, and two genuinely distinct granules sampled identically score as one. The correct merge score under the planned energy is a signed, comparable **ΔE = E(merged fit) − E(fit_i) − E(fit_j)**, which properly includes removing a hard-core pair term, the change in `π_k`, and the removal of `γ̃·A_ij` boundary area. The density ratio is unsigned, unnormalised, and bears no relation to the posterior.
2. **It only ever merges.** Our dominant error on this data is *under*-segmentation (the 446 z=1 components already contain merges). A monotone merge-only relaxation can only make the dominant error worse, and union-find offers no un-merge.
3. **No acceptance criterion.** Greedy-to-fixpoint with no way to escape a bad early merge.

**Recommendation (charter-compatible).** Revive only the scaffold, behind pluggable `score(pair) -> float` / `accept(score) -> bool`, with the default score a domain-neutral **energy delta**. The surfaced node behaviour is then "merge adjacent regions whose ΔE < 0" — a generic region-merging operator, with the granule energy supplied by the caller, so no granule-specific behaviour is exposed. Bonus: if `_adjacency_alpha` is replaced by the power/Set-Voronoi adjacency, the adjacency becomes **exact and parameter-free** and `adj_dist_um` disappears entirely.

**Two live defects worth logging while we're here:** `nodegraph/catalog/analysis/tessellate.py:57` pins `merge_tol = 0.0` (kernel default is 0.15, `adj_dist_um` 5.0); and `_tess_points` iterates `tess.boundaries` without skipping label `-1` — currently harmless because `NOISE_LABEL` in `granule_cluster.py` is defined but never emitted, but it will build a "boundary" for noise the moment the clusterer starts using it.

## Verdict

**Does a tessellation discriminate contacting granules better than the planned EDT watershed? There is therefore no cheaper alternative algorithm to adopt and no accuracy to gain by importing exact power-diagram machinery; it would buy implementation economy and an exact combinatorial adjacency graph, nothing more. What the measurement *does* justify is keeping the radius term (prefer D/F over B) and dropping any expectation that tessellation rescues the contacts.

### How it "blocks/tiles"

There are **two independent mechanisms, and only one of them is a bound**:

1. **Arena grouping** (`:81-86`) — a *candidate-set restriction*, not a geometric bound. A voxel of arena `a` competes only among seeds with `seed_arena == a`. The tree is rebuilt per arena. This is semantic, defined by `bound`.
2. **Voxel-block chunking** (`_VORONOI_BLOCK = 1 << 18` = 262 144, `:46`, loop `:88`) — pure *query-point* chunking to bound the peak `(rows, ndim) float64` coordinate array (`:43-45`). **Each block still queries the full tree of that arena's seeds.** No seed is ever pruned by blocking.
3. `min(s + _VORONOI_BLOCK, hi)` (`:94`) is load-bearing and commented as such (`:89-93`): `nz` is the concatenation of every arena's voxels, so an uncapped block hands the *next* arena's voxels to *this* arena's seeds.

### Could a per-seed weight turn this into a power diagram? — precise answer

**Yes, but `tree.query(pts, k=1)` cannot be reused for the argmin.** This is a rewrite of the inner loop, not a parameter addition.

`cKDTree.query` computes `argmin_k |x - c_k|`. A power diagram is `argmin_k (|x-c_k|² - R_k²)`. Minimizing power distance is equivalent to **maximizing** `2 c_k·x - |c_k|² + R_k²` — a max-inner-product / upper-envelope problem, not a nearest-neighbour problem in the original space. A KD-tree cannot answer it directly.

**How wrong is Euclidean on this bed? I measured it.** Synthetic RSA packing at the *measured* size distribution (lognormal fit to diameter p10=18/p50=35/p90=75/max=170 µm), N=213, radii 7.1–84.4 µm, area fraction 0.87, over 880 µm (= 512 px × 1.71828), on a 400² grid:

```
Euclidean argmin != POWER argmin      : 47.0% of voxels
Euclidean argmin != POWER argmin      : 48.5% of voxels INSIDE a granule
POWER argmin != APOLLONIUS argmin     :  3.4% overall,  0.0% inside a granule
Euclidean argmin != APOLLONIUS argmin : 50.2%
```

Two conclusions, both load-bearing for V2.22:

- **`analysis.voronoi` as shipped assigns roughly half the solid-phase voxels to the wrong granule on a polydisperse bed.** The 4× size range is exactly what breaks it. It is unusable as a discriminator without weights.
- **Inside the solid phase, the power diagram and the signed-radial-distance (Apollonius) argmin are identical — 0.0% disagreement.** So the planned energy's `argmin_k s_k` **is** a Laguerre tessellation on the voxels that get labelled. The claimed KEY PROPERTY holds, but the exact combinatorial object is the **Laguerre/power diagram, not the ordinary Voronoi**, and the decision surface is the **radical plane** (through the contact point of two tangent spheres), not the median plane between centroids — those coincide only for equal radii. Note that Apollonius (`argmin |x-c|-R`, curved bisectors, possibly non-convex cells) is the literal signed-distance argmin, and power (flat bisectors, convex cells, guaranteed tessellation) is what agrees with it inside the solid and is what the polydisperse-packing literature validates. Prefer power.

**Exact code cost in `_voronoi_assign`:**

- Add a `weights` (i.e. `R²`) parameter, aligned with `seeds_vox` rows.
- Replace `tree.query(pts, k=1, distance_upper_bound=upper)` (`:97`) with a two-stage candidate-then-rerank. The correct guarantee: for the power winner `c*`, `|x-c*|² - R*² ≤ d_nn² - R_min²` ⟹ **`|x-c*| ≤ sqrt(d_nn² + R_max² - R_min²)`**. I verified this bound holds for every voxel in both test configurations. Cost at the packed configuration above: `tree.query_ball_point` inside that radius returns **median 6, p95 9, max 12** candidates per voxel. A fixed-`k` `tree.query(pts, k=K)` + rerank also works with `K ≥ 10` there (power winner's Euclidean rank: p99 6, max 9) but `K` is data-dependent and silently wrong if under-set — prefer the guaranteed ball, or `K` with a re-query escalation.
- `max_distance_um` must move **after** the argmin: power distance is not a distance (it is negative inside the sphere), so it cannot be a `distance_upper_bound`. Compute the power argmin over unpruned candidates, then gate `|x - c_winner| > upper → unclaimed`. That preserves the documented meaning of the socket exactly (`:338-347`).

**Which socket carries the radius.** It must be a **per-seed column name**, not a scalar — the bed is 4× polydisperse and a scalar radius makes the power diagram collapse back to the Euclidean one (`R_k` constant ⟹ `argmin(|x-c|²-R²) = argmin|x-c|`). The right shape is a new `InString` with **no `layer_in`**, pointing at a column of the *same* Point layer already selected by `points` — exactly the precedent of `analysis.tessellate`'s `cluster` socket (`tessellate.py:204-211`: `InString("cluster", "Label column", field=False, default="cluster")`, no `layer_in`, so the GUI does not offer it as a layer picker). Something like `InString("radius_col", "Radius column", default="", ...)`, empty = unweighted (the current behaviour, so the baseline default stays semantically identical). It is read alongside the coordinate columns at `voronoi.py:200-212`, sliced by `sel` at `:255`, and must be converted to µm-consistent `R²` before the argmin. If the radii live on a LABEL table instead (e.g. an equivalent-radius column from `analysis.measure`), the caller joins them onto the Point table upstream — do not add a second layer socket.

**Does the blocking scheme survive a weighted metric? — Yes, both parts, and this is provable, not stylistic:**

- **Arena grouping survives.** It restricts the *candidate set* by `seed_arena`, and does not derive any distance bound. Restricting the candidate set is metric-agnostic: `argmin` over a subset is still exact for that subset, which is the defined semantics of `bound` (`:56-59`). Unchanged.
- **The 2^18 voxel block survives.** It chunks *query points*, not sites; every block sees the arena's whole tree. Exact for any metric. The `min(..., hi)` cap (`:94`) is about arena-run boundaries in `nz`, not about distance, so it stays correct verbatim.
- **`distance_upper_bound` does NOT survive** as an in-query prune, for the reason above. It is the only place a Euclidean-derived bound is used, and moving it after the argmin fixes it.

So the correctness answer is: **the pruning that would have been invalid is not in the blocking scheme; it is in `distance_upper_bound`, and the fix is to relocate it.**

### Is the adjacency reusable independently of the merge?

**Yes for both, and cheaply — they are free functions with no hidden state.**

- `_adjacency_voronoi(labels, ridge_points, active)` needs only `vor.ridge_points`, i.e. one `scipy.spatial.Voronoi` over the point cloud. Total cost = one Voronoi. Set Voronoi is the correct generalization of nearest-surface assignment to **non-spherical** objects, which is what you need once superquadrics are fitted (power diagrams are exact only for spheres). What is missing is (a) contact **area** per pair — `vor.ridge_vertices` is available and gives the ridge polygons, so this is an additive computation, not a rewrite; and (b) the union boundary is a `ConvexHull` of member points, documented as a first-cut approximation at `:270-271`.

### `skimage.segmentation.watershed` — flooding semantics (this is the leak answer)

From the installed docstring, Notes section, verbatim:

> *"The algorithm uses a priority queue to hold the pixels with the metric for the priority queue being **pixel value, then the time of entry into the queue** -- this settles ties in favor of the closest marker."*

**It floods by landscape VALUE, not by accumulated path cost.** Consequences for `Λ(v) = min_k s_k(v)`:

- A basin expands over every voxel whose *absolute* landscape value is low, regardless of how far the path from the seed ran. **There is no distance penalty and no barrier accumulation** — so a single low-valued channel of any length is a leak path, and a wide shallow saddle leaks before a narrow deep one. Since `min_k s_k` is very flat inside a large granule and only rises in the interstitial void, expect leaks across contacts where the void is thin — exactly the contacts that matter.
- Entry-time tiebreak makes plateaus split between markers on opposite sides (docstring: *"pixels on a plateau should be split between markers on opposite sides"*). This is why a flat `Λ` interior behaves like a distance split rather than degenerating.
- **`compactness` is the only thing that adds a seed-distance term.** Verified in the installed source: `watershed(image, markers=None, connectivity=1, offset=None, mask=None, compactness=0, watershed_line=False)` at `skimage/segmentation/_watershed.py:87-95`, passed straight into `_watershed_cy.watershed_raveled(...)` at `:230-239`. Compact watershed (Neubert & Protzel) adds `compactness · |x - seed|²` to the priority, which converts it from pure-value flooding to value-plus-spatial and is the shipped knob for suppressing leaks. Set it non-zero if you use the competitive-growth stage.
- `markers`: `int` (number of basins) or an int array where **zero means not a marker**. `None` ⇒ markers = `local_minima(image)` then `label(...)`; the docstring explicitly says *"users are encouraged to pass markers explicitly."*
- `mask`: bool/0-1 array of the same shape; **only points where `mask == True` are labelled**. This is your foreground gate — it is a hard restriction, not a cost, so it *does* stop leaks across it.
- `connectivity`: int ⇒ `scipy.ndimage.generate_binary_structure` max orthogonal steps (2D: 1 = 4-neighbourhood, 2 = 8); or an array used directly as a footprint. Default 1.
- `watershed_line=True` inserts a 1-px label-0 separator, with the documented caveat that *"the method used for adding this line expects that marker regions are not adjacent"* — for a dense bed with eroded-interior markers, adjacent markers are the normal case, so the line "may not catch borders between adjacent marker regions." Do not rely on it for the contact network.

### `skimage.morphology.h_maxima`

Confirmed present. Docstring: *"Determine all maxima of the image with height >= h"*; a maximum of height `h` requires a path to an equal-or-higher maximum whose minimal value is `f(M) - h`. Signature `h_maxima(image, h, footprint=None)`, default footprint = ball of radius 1 in the max norm (3×3 in 2D, 3×3×3 in 3D). Returns a **binary** image. **`h` is documented as "unsigned integer"** — on a float EDT you are relying on undocumented behaviour; scale the EDT to integer µm-tenths or verify explicitly. Note the footprint is in **voxels**, so on 23× anisotropic data a 3×3×3 ball is not a physical ball — another reason the operative case is per-plane 2D.

### `skimage.segmentation.expand_labels`

Confirmed present. *"Expand labels in label image by `distance` pixels without overlapping"* — each background pixel within Euclidean distance ≤ `distance` of a component takes that component's label; where several are in range, **the closest wins**. Has a **`spacing`** parameter (*"Spacing of elements along each dimension... used to specify the spacing rate of the distance transform"*), so it can be made physically correct on anisotropic data. Documented edge case: *"when a pixel has the same distance to multiple regions... the exact behavior depends on the upstream implementation of `scipy.ndimage.distance_transform_edt`"* — i.e. ties are unspecified. It is an **unweighted** nearest-label growth, so it has the same polydispersity flaw as the current `analysis.voronoi`: it cannot express a radius.

### Is there ANY direct power/Laguerre diagram in scipy or skimage?

**No. Definitively no.** I regex-scanned every `.py`/`.pyi` in both installed packages for `laguerre|power_diagram|power diagram|weighted voronoi|additively weighted` (case-insensitive): **0 hits in skimage, 0 hits in scipy.** No API, no keyword, no private helper.

**But the exact combinatorial machinery IS available, via lifting, and I verified it.** Lift each site to `(c_k, |c_k|² - R_k²)` in `R^{d+1}`, take the **lower** convex hull (`ConvexHull.equations[:, -1] < 0`); its projection is the regular (weighted Delaunay) triangulation, the exact dual of the power diagram. On the packed polydisperse configuration from §1 (N=213, area fraction 0.87):

```
lifted-hull pairs 620 | brute-force raster power-cell adjacency 557
raster pairs NOT in lifted hull: 0        <-- exact superset, nothing missed
sites with EMPTY power cell: 0 | of those present in lower hull: 0
```

So: **one `scipy.spatial.ConvexHull` in d+1 dimensions gives the exact contact network of the power tessellation**, with zero false negatives, and automatically excludes sites whose sphere is entirely buried. That is the "much cheaper algorithm than per-voxel field evaluation" the question suspected exists — `O(n log n)` on ~350 sites instead of `O(voxels × candidates)` on 1M voxels. Cell volumes/areas still need either the raster or explicit halfspace intersection, but the **adjacency graph and the merge/relaxation decisions do not touch the raster at all.**

## 6. Where does the adjacency graph live? — decisive recommendation

### What the 11 domains offer (`nodegraph/domains.py`)

Seven lattice domains (`_LATTICE_AXES`, `:178-186`) are coarsenings over `{m,t,z,c,y,x}` — none can express a *pair of objects*; their identity IS an axis tuple. Four structure domains (`STRUCTURE_DOMAINS`, `:200-202`): LABEL, POINT, TRACK, MESH. `MULTI_INSTANCE_DOMAINS = STRUCTURE_DOMAINS` (`:206`), so any of them can hold several tables keyed by layer name.

### How the repo already models a relation — the decisive precedent

**`Domain.TRACK`/`TrackMembership` is a pair table stored under an existing structure domain.** `structure.py:295-330`:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class TrackMembership:
    track_id: np.ndarray
    t: np.ndarray
    member_id: np.ndarray
    member_domain: Domain = Domain.LABEL
    ...
    def to_table(self, *, layer=None) -> StructureTable:
        return StructureTable(Domain.TRACK, {
            "track_id": self.track_id, "t": self.t, "member_id": self.member_id,
        }, layer=layer, z_kind="plane_index")
```

Three parallel int columns, **one row per relation edge**, no geometry. Written by `catalog/track/link.py:87`: `return ds.with_structure(membership.to_table(layer=out_layer))`.

**Critically: this table has no `id`, no `z`, no `y`, no `x`.** `COORD_COLUMNS = ("id","m","t","c","z","y","x")` (`structure.py:39`) is described as invariant, but I traced every use: `nodegraph/__init__.py:51,110` (re-export), `catalog/transform/transfer_structure.py:164-167` (a **reserved-output-name** check, not a schema validator), `selftest.py:4019` and `:5762` (assertions on LABEL/POINT/MESH tables only). **There is no runtime validator.** `Dataset.with_structure` (`dataset.py:246-271`) just explodes `table.columns` into per-column `AttributeLayer`s keyed by `(domain, name, layer)` and stores `z_kind` in `__struct_zkind__`. The Track fixture in `test_transfer_structure` (`selftest.py:9396-9402`) has columns `track_id, t, member_id, m, c, tlen` — no `id/z/y/x` — and every assertion passes. So **a pair table is already a legal, shipped, tested shape** in this engine, and `nodelab_v2/spreadsheet.py` (which sorts by `_COORD_ORDER`, `:41`) already displays one.

The second precedent, for the *summary* half: **`analysis.object_metrics` already collapses a neighbour relation onto per-object columns** — `neighbor_dist_mean`, `neighbor_dist_std` (`object_metrics.py:28`, `:241-242`), written with `with_layer` **not** `with_structure` (`:257`: *"with_layer (not with_structure): adds columns to the EXISTING member layer without…"*).

### Recommendation — be decisive: two tables, no new domain

**(c) the adjacency graph lives as a second `StructureTable` on `Domain.LABEL`, under a derived layer name, modelled byte-for-byte on `TrackMembership.to_table()`.**

```
StructureTable(Domain.LABEL,
    {"a": int64, "b": int64,           # a < b, canonical, one row per unordered pair
     "contact_area_um2": float,        # the per-pair value
     "m": int64, "t": int64, "c": int64},
    layer=f"{name}_contacts", z_kind="plane_index")   # "subpixel" in 3D
```

written with `ds.with_structure(...)`, plus — separately — **per-object summary columns on the existing LABEL table** (`n_contacts`, `contact_area_um2`, `coordination`) via `with_layer`, exactly the `object_metrics` pattern. The two together are what every consumer actually wants: the summary columns join on `id` for Measure/plotting with zero new machinery, and the pair table carries the graph for the merge/relaxation loop.

The layer must be announced by an `extra_layers` callable (`registry.py:358-365`: *"Layers this node creates that no `layer_out` socket can describe… derive the name from ANOTHER param"* — precisely `analysis.extract_boundary`'s `f"{labels}_boundary"` case), because `layer_out` on the `name` socket already claims `(VOXEL, LABEL)` under the base name.

### Why not a new domain — the cost, itemized against zero benefit

A `Domain.CONTACT` would require, in one commit:

1. `Domain` enum member (`domains.py:49-65`).
2. `DOMAIN_COLOR` (`:74-90`) — the comment at `:85-88` says this is **MANDATORY in the same commit**: *"`nodelab_v2.theme` builds its QColor map by iterating `Domain`, so a missing color used to KeyError at GUI import."* And it must not collide with the eleven existing hexes.
3. `DOMAIN_ABBR` (`:92-97`), `DOMAIN_DOC` (`:107-145`).
4. `STRUCTURE_DOMAINS` (`:200-202`) — membership is what unlocks `is_structure`, the spreadsheet column gate, and CSV/Arrow export (`:197-199`). Omit it and the domain is invisible; include it and it becomes multi-instance too (`:206`).
5. A `BRIDGE_FUNCS` entry (`bridges.py:345-358`) — **and here is the fatal problem.** Every one of the 12 existing bridges is either a `_group_reduce` (gather) or a paint/broadcast keyed by a *single* id. A pair has **two** ids, so `CONTACT → LABEL` is ambiguous by construction (does object `a`'s value come from row `(a,b)` or `(b,a)`? both, summed? maxed?) — the reduction must be *chosen*, which means it is a **node parameter, not a bridge**. There is no well-defined default rule to put on a wire, which is the entire premise of the bridge model.
6. `test_transfer_structure` (`selftest.py:9345-9347`) asserts **exact set equality** on `from_domain`/`to_domain` choices. A 5th structure domain either breaks those two assertions or must be added to both, and then the *"matrix is exactly the 15 pairs"* contract (`:9326`) requires defining or explicitly refusing **9 new pairs** (4 sources × contact, contact × 5 targets, minus the self-pair) each with a named-alternative refusal message (`:9511-9531`).

Benefit purchased: none that the LABEL-layer pair table does not already provide. The pair table gets the spreadsheet, the content hash (`structure.py:60-67`), Arrow export (`:69-80`), `z_kind` provenance, and multi-instance layering **for free**, because `Domain.LABEL` is already in `STRUCTURE_DOMAINS`.

### Why not columns-on-LABEL alone

Because contact area is per-**pair**, not per-object, and the pair count is variable. Encoding it as columns forces either a fixed-width `neighbor_0..neighbor_k` (arbitrary `k`, and `object_metrics`'s `n_neighbors` default of 6 is already documented as *"roughly the contact number of a confluent monolayer"* — a polydisperse bed's coordination number varies with radius, so a fixed `k` biases against exactly the size-dependence you are trying to measure) or a lossy scalar summary. Keep both: the pair table for the graph, the summary columns for the join. That is the same split the engine already ships for tracks — `TrackMembership` for the relation, `tlen`/`speed` columns for the per-object view.

### One caveat to design around

`catalog/_shared/labels.py:47-58` treats "has a LABEL table under this name" as the signal "this Voxel raster is a label raster." Writing `f"{name}_contacts"` on `Domain.LABEL` makes `structure_zkind(LABEL, f"{name}_contacts")` non-`None` while **no Voxel raster of that name exists** — so `_label_raster` fails on its *first* check (`:39-46`, "no Voxel layer …") with the correct message, not a false positive. Verified by reading the function. But the GUI's layer pickers for `layer_in=Domain.LABEL` sockets will list `{name}_contacts` as a selectable Label instance, and it has no `id` column. That is the same wart `tracks` already has, so it is consistent rather than new — worth a naming convention (a trailing `_contacts`) rather than a code change.

## 4.1 `nodegraph/kernels/granule_cluster.py`

**(a) The silent-fallback dispatch is gone.** `fit_one = _fit_kmeans if method == "kmeans"
else _fit_gmm` ran the GMM for *any* unrecognised method string. With more than two entries
that is a correctness bug no test can see: a caller asking for `dbscan` got plausible
numbers from the wrong model. Replaced by a module-level `_FITTERS` dict plus an explicit
`ValueError` naming the menu.

**(b) The discarded orientation and responsibilities are now surfaced.** scikit-learn was
already computing both and they were being thrown away. `info` gains:

| key | shape | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `resp` | `(N, k)` float32 | `predict_proba` for the GMM; a one-hot for KMeans, which is hard-assigning by construction rather than faking a soft value |
| `covariances` | `(k, 3, 3)` or `None` | `None` for the spherical model |
| `axes` | `(k, 3, 3)` or `None` | unit eigenvectors as COLUMNS, descending eigenvalue, flipped to `det = +1` (a reflection is not a rotation) |
| `axis_lengths_um` | `(k, 3)` or `None` | **full** axis lengths |

Extras are computed for the **winning** `k` only — doing it per candidate would allocate an
`(N, k)` responsibility matrix per sweep step for nothing. The fit helpers now return a
`_Fit` NamedTuple carrying the estimator so this needs no refit.

**The sqrt(5) factor is load-bearing and is verified.** A uniformly-filled solid ellipsoid
with semi-axis `a` has second moment `lambda = a^2/5`, so `a = sqrt(5*lambda)`. Fitting a
GAUSSIAN to points drawn from such a solid under-reports the extent by `sqrt(5) = 2.236x`
without it. Round-trip check: a uniform solid with true full-lengths `[60, 24, 10]` um
recovers `[60.08, 23.97, 10.01]`.

Two caveats are documented in the code, because they make `axes` a good *initialiser* and a
poor final answer: the sqrt(5) correction assumes a uniformly filled solid, and **at a
contact the point cloud is truncated**, so the covariance is biased and the Gaussian
systematically under-reports that object's size.

**(c) `NOISE_LABEL` finally means something.** New `noise_resp` param (default `0.0` = off,
so historical behaviour is unchanged): points whose top responsibility falls below it are
relabelled `-1`. Verified monotone on deliberately overlapping clusters — 0/400 rejected at
0.0, 55 at 0.6, 271 at 0.9, 399 at 0.999.

## 4.4 `nodegraph/catalog/_shared/objects.py` — the unblocking

Before this, `_object_table` refused **every** `z_kind == "subpixel"` table, and

## 4.5 `analysis.object_metrics` — per-metric refusal

Passes `allow_3d=True` and refuses only `divergence` and `curl`
(`_OBJECT_METRICS_PLANAR`), which is the `analysis.measure` precedent — it rejects just its
2D-only shape entries rather than the whole volume. A 2-D curl is a scalar where a 3-D one is
a vector, and the divergence here is fitted from an in-plane neighbourhood, so neither has a
correct volumetric answer.

`velocity` writes `vz` **only** on a volumetric table (an all-NaN column on every 2-D
workflow is noise), and `speed` becomes the 3-norm only there — folding an all-NaN `vz` into
the 2-D case would turn every existing `speed` column into NaN.

`z_step_um` is read **only** when the table is volumetric, so a 2-D pull is not memo-fenced
on a calibration key it could never have consumed — the rule `_frame_interval_s` already
states for `dt_s`. Using `pixel_size_um` for the axial scale would silently report a lateral
scale on an axis 23x coarser.

Driving coverage toward 1.0 — i.e. fixing the 93 "too tight" marks — **destroys detection**,
because as the mask loosens *more material becomes fewer objects*: neighbours fuse, and one blob
gets one label. A threshold answers "is this material" and is then asked to settle "where does
one object end", which it has no evidence about. **No better-calibrated threshold escapes this**
— p(void) *is* the better-calibrated threshold.

## THE PROBABILITY MAP, AS THE USER SPECIFIED IT 2026-08-06 (`CodeLog/live` e067)

Four probability spaces over one field, which compose into a single discrimination:

| # | space | status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **p(granule vs void)**, per pixel | a hard Otsu threshold, and it FRAGMENTS. Must become `E_0` from the energy — the background term the plan already calls load-bearing and nothing has used |
| 2 | **p(F vs I)**, per granule | strongest of the four: channel dominance agreed with the user's eye on 148 of 149 |
| 3 | **p(which granule)**, per pixel | calibrated. ECE 0.064, ceiling ~0.55, expected-count error 0.3% |
| 4 | **p(cell)**, per pixel | DOES NOT EXIST. 59% recall at 24% precision on M01 against the 134 hand-drawn circles |

**The composition, in the user's words:** a cell in the *gap* between two granules at a
contested boundary raises the confidence that the boundary is real, because cells occupy the
interstitial space. With the explicit caveat that a cell merely on a granule's *surface* must
not be used to split it — or every F granule, which carries cells on its surface by design,
would be torn apart.

**The build order this forces, which is not the obvious one.** The caveat is enforced by asking
space 1 whether the cell is in the void or on a body — so its correctness depends on space 1
being reliable, and space 1 is currently a fragmenting binary threshold. A speckled foreground
mis-files surface cells as interstitial, which is exactly the failure the caveat exists to
prevent. **So p(void) must land before the cell detector is worth building.** Order: `E_0` as a
probability → cell detector calibrated on the 134 circles → the composition → re-test the
contested borders against the marks.

**Measured, and too weak to use yet:** a void cell within 6 µm sits at 1.3% of real borders and
0.0% of the 159 spurious ones — right direction, zero false positives, far too rare, and
meaningless at 24% detector precision. A *surface* cell within 10 µm sits at 16.9% vs 7.5%, a
2.2× lift with six times the coverage; mechanistically defensible (a real divide has two
surfaces meeting, a spurious one has none) but it is the quantity the user cautioned against,
and awaiting their call on the F-population confound.
